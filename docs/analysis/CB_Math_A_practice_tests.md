# A. College Board Digital SAT Math — Style DNA from Official Practice Tests 4–7 (Math sections)

Source: 4 official practice-test math sections (paper-adapted digital SAT forms), read as extracted text AND page images (figures verified visually). All content below is structural/statistical; no item text, answer sets, or figures are reproduced.

## 0. STRUCTURAL CAVEAT (important, verified by count)
- These PDFs are the **paper-and-pencil adaptations** of the digital SAT practice tests: **27 questions per module**, not 22. Total ledger = **8 modules × 27 = 216 items** (not 176).
- Every module = **20 MC + 7 SPR**, with SPR at **fixed positions 6, 7, 13, 14, 20, 21, 27** (identical in all 8 modules — verified on page images).
- Module 2 is **NOT harder** than Module 1 here (linear form, no adaptivity): each module runs its own easy→hard ramp. The Bluebook adaptive version has 22 Q/module with SPR interspersed less rigidly; blueprint percentages below still transfer.
- Calculator allowed throughout; identical reference sheet precedes each module; SPR direction sheet precedes each module (answers ≤5 chars positive / ≤6 negative, fractions or decimals allowed, no symbols).

Legend — Domain: ALG (Algebra), ADV (Advanced Math), PSDA (Problem-Solving & Data Analysis), GEO (Geometry & Trig). Diff: E/M/H (estimated from position + solution complexity; no metadata exists in PDFs). Fmt: MC/SPR. Visual: stimulus visual (choice-only visuals flagged). Context: abstract vs applied + 2-word topic.

## 3. CONVENTIONS

### (a) Canonical stem formulas (frequencies over 216)
- "What is the value of ___ ?" — ≈44 items (20%); the workhorse for SPR and hard MC; target is often a composite (a+b, bc, 7p, 30x, 39x, 72x, 6(x−2), x−7, n+4, k/x).
- "Which of the following …" — ≈55 items (~25%; ~34% of MC).
- "The function f is defined by f(x) = … ." opener — ≈16 items; always names the function then gives the rule on its own displayed line or inline.
- "Which equation/function represents [this situation] / defines [line t, circle B, f]?" — ≈11.
- "Which expression is equivalent to … ?" — 9 (this is the equivalent-expressions stem, nearly 1:1).
- "What is the best interpretation of ___ in this context?" — 9 (always exactly this wording).
- "The solution to the given system of equations is (x, y). What is the value of ___ ?" — 9 (4 of them ask for a scaled/shifted target, not x or y).
- "What is a solution / one possible solution / the positive solution to the given equation?" — 8 (signals multi-root items; SPR versions accept any listed root).
- "In the xy-plane, …" opener — ≈14; "The graph of … is shown." — ≈15.
- "… is shown. / The [table/scatterplot/bar graph/dot plot] shows …" — every visual item opens by naming its exhibit type.
- Displayed equation above stem with zero preamble (equation floats first, then "The given equation …") — ≈25 items.
- Parenthetical clarifiers, always in round brackets at stem end: unit conversions "(1 mile = 1,760 yards)", "(Use 1 mile = 1,609 meters.)", answer-format "(Express your answer as a decimal or fraction, not as a percent.)", assumption "(Assume there is no sales tax.)" — 5 occurrences total; used ONLY when ambiguity is real.
- Constants introduced as "…, where a and b are constants." / "k is a constant. …" — ~30 items; parameter letters draw from a,b,c,k,p,r,s,t,w and never collide with axis variables in the same item.

### (b) Answer-choice conventions
- Always exactly 4 options A)–D); one correct; no "None of the above."
- Numeric options sorted monotonically — ascending in ~90% of numeric sets, occasionally descending (radical/geometry sets); never shuffled.
- Distractors are **derivations, not noise**: each wrong option is reachable by one nameable error (sign slip, skipped step, wrong target, role swap, stem-number echo). E-item sets usually contain the stem's own numbers verbatim.
- Equation options share one surface template varying a single slot (sign, coefficient placement, exponent position, (y−k) vs (y+k)).
- Interpretation items: 4 full-sentence options, grammatically parallel, near-equal length (no longest-is-correct tell); each distractor is a systematic mis-mapping (slope↔intercept, input↔output, amount↔rate, x↔y of a vertex).
- Special formats: solution-count set "Zero / Exactly one / Exactly two / Infinitely many" (2×); Roman-numeral "I only / II only / I and II / Neither I nor II" (1×); "It must increase/decrease by …" paired verbs (1×); "could be" vs "must be" wording is load-bearing (6×).
- Typography: stacked fractions; π kept symbolic (e.g., 726π); radicals simplified except when the unsimplified form IS the trap; thousands commas everywhere (incl. 5-digit answers); degree symbols on angle options; coordinates as full ordered pairs.

### (c) SPR answer characteristics (n=56)
- Integers ≈ 70% (39/56); fractions ≈ 23% (e.g., 1/5, 3/10, 15/17, 11/28, 44/3, 7/24, 11/4, 361/8); decimal-natural ≈ 7% (7.5, 4.41, 37.8, −6.5, 4.51).
- Negatives: 2 required-negative answers (a "no-solution slope" parameter; a root-midpoint), 3 further items where a negative root is an acceptable alternate. So ~4–9% negative.
- Magnitude range observed: 0.2 → 4,205; several deliberate 3–4-digit answers (774, 336, 600, 1677, 2850, 4176, 4205, 1660) prove SPR ≠ small numbers; everything fits the 5-char (6 with minus) rule; repeating decimals are authored as fractions that fit (44/3).
- Multi-answer SPR: absolute-value / factored-polynomial items accept any single valid root (3 items).
- Easy SPRs (Q6/7/13/14) are one- or two-step with clean integers; hard SPRs (Q20/21/27) produce fractions, negatives, or large integers via parameter reasoning.

### (d) Number aesthetics
- Coefficients: small integers dominate (|k| ≤ 30 in ~85% of algebra items). Decimals appear only where the context earns them (prices 4.51/6.07, rates 0.6/1.8, probabilities 0.48/0.24/0.49, growth bases 1.02/1.034/1.04/1.84/0.64/0.1/2.9).
- Hard items deliberately use "ugly-but-engineered" constants that collapse cleanly: −99/7 and 65/7 hiding integer a; 1,089√3 hiding 33; 92/47·K hiding s=8; 10√37/24√37 hiding 5-12-13; 896.86 hiding coefficient-read; 4^(8c)=∛(4⁷) hiding 7/24.
- Pythagorean triples recycled constantly, usually scaled: 3-4-5, 8-15-17 (×3), 5-12-13 (×2√37), 12-35-37 (×2).
- Percent values are integers (6, 14, 20, 25, 190, 400); angles are integers; π and radicals never decimalized.
- Answers to E/M items are almost always integers; "clean" is a property of the ANSWER, not necessarily the givens.

### (e) Context register
- Applied ≈ 30% of items (63/216); abstract/math-native ≈ 70%. By domain: PSDA ~85% applied, ALG ~40%, ADV ~20%, GEO ~10%.
- Named people: 9/216 items (4%). Single given-name only, culturally diverse (e.g., Hana, Nasir, Kaylani, Tavon, Zinah, Lorenzo pattern), never two people, no dialogue, no cuteness.
- Context length: 1–3 sentences, purely functional; typical applied stem ≤ 45 words; the longest stimulus in 216 items is ~110 words (a physics-flux item). No humor, no backstory, no brand names; institutions are generic ("a company", "a store", "a state park", "a certain city").
- Topic palette: small commerce (prices, coupons, fees, rentals), school/club logistics, biology counts (turtles, raccoons, chipmunks, bacteria — often with one Latin species name per form), light physics (pressure, kinetic energy, height of dropped/launched object), civic data (votes, samples). Money is the most common unit.
- Units: dollars dominant; imperial and metric both used freely (inches/feet/yards/miles/pounds vs cm/m/kg/kelvins/°C/psi/joules); any needed conversion factor is GIVEN in parentheses — never assumed knowledge.
- Realistic-data flavor: numbers are plausible but not researched (a fish swims 5,104 yards); time series use plain year ranges; decimals mimic real prices/rates.

### (f) Figure conventions
- Coordinate graphs: full fine gridlines at unit (or labeled) spacing; both axes arrowed; italic x, y at arrow tips; origin marked O; tick numbers every 1–2 units (or 2,000 etc. with commas); negative quadrants shown only when used. Context graphs replace x/y with titled axes: "Time (seconds)", "Charge (kWh)", "Temperature (kelvins)" — always Unit-in-parentheses.
- Geometry figures: minimal line art, no shading; right-angle box marks; italic capital vertex letters; side lengths as bare numerals; angle values with °; **"Note: Figure not drawn to scale." appears under 7 of 7 geometry figures** (plural "Figures" when two). Coordinate grids never carry the note.
- Tables: boxed cells, centered values, header row either variable names (x | f(x)) or category labels; two-way tables include a Total row/column; answer-choice tables are miniature versions of the same style.
- Scatterplots: solid dots, optional best-fit straight line; ~7–12 points; dot plot uses stacked dots over an integer number line with a title above.
- Answer-choice graphs: 4 identically-scaled mini-grids labeled A)–D).

## 5. ESSENCE — what a College Board math item actually is
1. One item = one insight. Every item isolates exactly one mathematical decision (a swap, a structure, a condition); all arithmetic around it is deliberately frictionless.
2. The difficulty lives in the SETUP, never in messy numbers: hard items use engineered constants (65/7, 1,089√3, 24√37) that collapse the moment the right structure is seen.
3. Distractors are the wrong turns of the intended solution path — each one is derivable, nameable, and instructive. If you can't say which specific error produces option C, it isn't a CB item.
4. Stems are ruthlessly economical: median stem ≈ 25 words; declarative setup sentences, then a single interrogative ending in "?"; no motivation, no story arc, no second question.
5. Self-containment is absolute: conversion factors, formulas beyond the reference sheet, species names, and answer-format expectations are handed over in parentheses; nothing tests trivia.
6. Contexts are dry and interchangeable: a named person does at most one economic or observational act. The math would survive deleting the context — and the context never decorates abstract items (70% have none).
7. The register is flat and hedged: "estimated", "approximately", "best interpretation", "could be", "must be" are load-bearing; nothing is exclamatory.
8. Wrong-target questions ("what is 39x?") are a signature: they reward manipulating the SYSTEM as an object instead of grinding for x — prep-company clones almost never do this.
9. Interpretation items test mapping, not computing: all four options are true-sounding sentences built from the same two numbers with permuted roles.
10. Parameters (a, b, c, k, p, t, w) are first-class citizens: roughly 1 item in 6 asks about a constant's value or consequence, not the variable's.
11. Geometry above easy level goes figure-less; when a figure exists it is austere, and it ALWAYS carries "Note: Figure not drawn to scale." if metric conclusions could be eyeballed.
12. Format rhythm is fixed: 20 MC + 7 SPR at 6/7, 13/14, 20/21, 27; ascending numeric choices; exactly four options; equation displayed above a "The given equation…" stem.
13. The difficulty ramp is honest and monotone (E through ~Q9, M through ~Q18, H from ~Q21), with Q27 always a hard SPR closer.
14. SPR answers prove range discipline: mostly clean integers, but unafraid of 15/17, 7/24, −6.5, or 4,205 — always ≤5 characters.
