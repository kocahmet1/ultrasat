Two-Variable Data

Two-variable data is a set of pairs—hours studied and score earned, temperature and sales. A scatterplot plots each pair as a point, and the cloud's shape reveals the association between the variables:

Positive association
Negative association
No clear association

Strategies

  • Read the axes first—what each variable is, and in what units. Half the wrong answers misuse the axes.
  • The line of best fit summarizes the trend. Predictions come from the line, not from individual points.
  • Slope = predicted change in $y$ per one unit of $x$; the y-intercept is the prediction at $x = 0$. Interpret both with the axes' units.
  • Actual $-$ predicted: a point above the line beats its prediction; below the line falls short.
  • Association is a tendency, not a law—and it is not causation. The right answers say “tends to” and “predicted.”
Every scatterplot question is one of four jobs: describe the trend, interpret the line's numbers, predict from the line, or compare a point with the line. Identify the job before touching the plot.
Problem-Solving and Data AnalysisTwo-Variable Data: Read the Scatterplot

Read the Scatterplot

Besides its direction, a cloud of points has a shape. If the trend runs straight, a linear model fits; if it bends, you'll need a curve:

Linear trend
Nonlinear trend
Example

Which statement best describes the relationship shown in the scatterplot?

  1. A)As TV time increases, test scores tend to decrease
  2. B)As TV time increases, test scores tend to increase
  3. C)There is no relationship between TV time and test scores
  4. D)Watching more TV causes lower test scores
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: The cloud falls from left to right—a negative association, described with “tends to.”
  • Choice B: That would slope upward; this cloud clearly falls.
  • Choice C: “No relationship” looks like a shapeless cloud—this one has an unmistakable downward drift.
  • Choice D: The data shows the two variables move together—it cannot show that one causes the other. Association is never causation on the SAT.
Correct answers about scatterplots hedge: “tend to,” “in general,” “predicted.” Choices with “causes,” “always,” or “every” overstate what data can say—and they're nearly always wrong.
Problem-Solving and Data AnalysisTwo-Variable Data: The Line of Best Fit

The Line of Best Fit

The line of best fit runs through the middle of the cloud—roughly half the points above, half below—and turns the trend into an equation you can interpret and use. Here, quiz score $y$ against hours studied $x$, fit by $\hat{y} = 4x + 55$:

Number in the modelMeaning in context
slope $= 4$predicted increase of $4$ points for each additional hour studied
y-intercept $= 55$predicted score for a student who studies $0$ hours
Interpretations always carry the axes' units — points per hour, points at zero hours.
Example

Based on the model $\hat{y} = 4x + 55$, which of the following is the best interpretation of the number $4$?

  1. A)The predicted increase in quiz score for each additional hour studied
  2. B)The predicted quiz score for a student who does not study
  3. C)The number of hours a typical student studies
  4. D)The exact number of points every student gains per hour of studying
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: The slope multiplies $x$ (hours), so it's the predicted change in $y$ (points) per one hour.
  • Choice B: That's the y-intercept's job—the $55$.
  • Choice C: $4$ carries units of points per hour, not hours—the units veto this reading.
  • Choice D: “Exact… every student” claims certainty a trend line doesn't have. Models predict on average; the scattered points prove individuals vary.
Best-fit interpretations have a fixed grammar: slope → “predicted change in [y-units] per one [x-unit]”; intercept → “predicted [y] when [x] is zero.” Match the choice to the grammar and the units.
Problem-Solving and Data AnalysisTwo-Variable Data: Predictions & the Gap to the Line

Predictions & the Gap to the Line

Predicting from the Model

To predict, feed an $x$-value to the line—the points have already done their job by shaping it.

Example

Using $\hat{y} = 4x + 55$, what score does the model predict for a student who studies $6$ hours?

$$\hat{y} = 4(6) + 55$$
Substitute $x = 6$ into the model
$$\hat{y} = 79$$
The predicted score

Actual vs. Predicted

Real students don't land exactly on the line. The vertical gap—actual $-$ predicted—says who beat the model and by how much. One student studied $6$ hours and scored $85$:

Rule: Point above the line → actual $>$ predicted (difference positive). Point below → actual $<$ predicted (negative). “Farthest from the line” means the largest vertical gap, sign ignored.

Example

For which point does the actual $y$-value differ most from the value predicted by the line of best fit?

  1. A)A
  2. B)B
  3. C)C
  4. D)D
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Predicted $2 + 2 = 4$, actual $7$: a gap of $3$.
  • Choice B: Predicted $7$, actual $6$: a gap of only $1$.
  • Choice C: Predicted $10$, actual $14$: a gap of $4$—the largest vertical distance from the line.
  • Choice D: Predicted $11$, actual $10$: a gap of $1$. Being far to the right isn't being far from the line.
Problem-Solving and Data AnalysisTwo-Variable Data: Choosing the Model

Choosing the Model

When the SAT asks which kind of model fits, it's a shape-recognition task—the same fingerprints from the Nonlinear Functions lesson, now worn by data:

Linear
Quadratic
Exponential

Rule: From a table with equal $x$-steps: constant differences → linear model; constant ratios → exponential model; differences that shrink then grow (or a visible turn) → quadratic.

Example
$x$$y$
$0$$3$
$1$$6$
$2$$12$
$3$$24$

The table shows values from a data set. Which type of model best fits the data?

  1. A)Increasing exponential
  2. B)Increasing linear
  3. C)Decreasing exponential
  4. D)Quadratic with a minimum
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Each step doubles $y$: ratios $\frac{6}{3} = \frac{12}{6} = \frac{24}{12} = 2$. Constant ratio, growing values—exponential growth, $y = 3 \cdot 2^x$.
  • Choice B: Linear needs constant differences; these are $3, 6, 12$—growing, not constant.
  • Choice C: The values rise; decay falls.
  • Choice D: A quadratic with a minimum would fall before it rises—these values only climb, and by ratios, not by a turning pattern.
Growth by adding (“$120$ more members each year”) → linear. Growth by multiplying (“increases $8\%$ each year”, “doubles”) → exponential. The verbs in the question tell you the model.
Problem-Solving and Data AnalysisTwo-Variable Data: Practice

Practice

Name the job—trend, interpretation, prediction, gap-to-line, or model choice—then keep units and “predicted” language in view.

Practice
  1. 1.

    Which statement about the data in the scatterplot is true?

    1. A)As temperature increases, sales tend to decrease
    2. B)As temperature increases, sales tend to increase
    3. C)Temperature and sales show no association
    4. D)Selling less hot chocolate causes the temperature to rise

    The correct answer is A. The cloud falls steadily from left to right—warm days, few cups.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: A clear negative association, stated as a tendency. ✓
    • Choice B: The trend runs the other way.
    • Choice C: The downward drift is unmistakable—“no association” would look like a formless cloud.
    • Choice D: Backwards and causal: the data can't establish causation in either direction—it only shows the variables moving together.
  2. 2.

    The model $h = 12w + 30$ predicts the height $h$, in millimeters, of a seedling $w$ weeks after planting. Which is the best interpretation of the number $12$ in this context?

    1. A)The predicted increase in height, in millimeters, each week
    2. B)The height of the seedling, in millimeters, when it was planted
    3. C)The predicted height, in millimeters, after one week
    4. D)The number of weeks it takes the seedling to double in height

    The correct answer is A. The $12$ multiplies $w$ (weeks), so its units are millimeters per week—the slope's predicted weekly growth.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Slope grammar: predicted change in $h$ per one unit of $w$. ✓
    • Choice B: The height at planting ($w = 0$) is the intercept, $30$.
    • Choice C: The predicted height after one week is $12(1) + 30 = 42$ mm—the whole expression, not the $12$ alone.
    • Choice D: $12$ is in millimeters per week, not weeks—units rule it out.
  3. 3.

    A line of best fit for a data set is $\hat{y} = 3x + 20$. One of the data points is $(10, 44)$. What is the actual $y$-value minus the value predicted by the model at $x = 10$?

    1. A)$-6$
    2. B)$6$
    3. C)$44$
    4. D)$50$

    The correct answer is A.

    $$\hat{y} = 3(10) + 20 = 50$$
    Predicted value at $x = 10$
    $$44 - 50 = -6$$
    Actual minus predicted — the point sits $6$ below the line
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Negative, because the actual value fell short of the prediction. ✓
    • Choice B: $+6$ subtracts in the wrong order—sign carries meaning here (above vs. below the line).
    • Choice C: $44$ is the actual value alone; the question asks for the comparison.
    • Choice D: $50$ is the predicted value alone—one input to the subtraction, not its result.
  4. 4.
    $x$$y$
    $0$$80$
    $1$$40$
    $2$$20$
    $3$$10$

    Which type of model best fits the data in the table?

    1. A)Decreasing exponential
    2. B)Decreasing linear
    3. C)Increasing exponential
    4. D)Quadratic with a maximum

    The correct answer is A. Each step halves $y$: ratios of $\frac{1}{2}$ throughout—constant ratio, shrinking values. The model is $y = 80 \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^x$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Constant ratio $\frac{40}{80} = \frac{20}{40} = \frac{10}{20} = \frac{1}{2}$. ✓
    • Choice B: Linear decay needs equal drops; these drops are $40, 20, 10$—shrinking, not constant.
    • Choice C: The values fall; growth rises.
    • Choice D: A quadratic with a maximum rises before it falls—this data only falls, and by ratios.

Things to Remember

  • Read the axes and units before anything else. Association: cloud rises → positive, falls → negative, formless → none.
  • The line of best fit is the trend's summary—predict from the line, not from individual points.
  • Slope: predicted change in $y$ per one unit of $x$. Y-intercept: predicted $y$ at $x = 0$. Interpretations must match the units and say “predicted.”
  • Actual $-$ predicted: positive above the line, negative below; “farthest from the line” = largest vertical gap.
  • Model choice: equal differences → linear; equal ratios → exponential; a turn (peak or valley) → quadratic.
  • Association is never causation, and trends are tendencies—reject choices with “causes,” “exactly,” or “every.”
