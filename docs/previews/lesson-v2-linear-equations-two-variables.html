Linear Equations in Two Variables

A linear equation in two variables relates an $x$ and a $y$, each to the first power. Unlike a one-variable equation, it doesn't have a single answer: a solution is an ordered pair $(x, y)$ that makes the equation true, and there are infinitely many of them. Plotted together, those pairs form a straight line—the equation and the line are two descriptions of the same object.

You'll meet these equations in two standard costumes:

Slope-intercept form
$$y = \textcolor{#0f6cbd}{m}x + \textcolor{#157f4b}{b}$$

Built for graphs and rates: the slope and y-intercept are visible at a glance.

Standard form
$$\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{A}x + \textcolor{#0e8a7e}{B}y = \textcolor{#157f4b}{C}$$

Built for totals and combinations: two quantities contributing to one result.

Strategies

  • A solution is a point on the line. To test a pair, substitute both coordinates; a true statement means it's a solution.
  • Match the form to the job. Need slope or a graph? Solve for $y$. Need a total or intercepts? Standard form is faster.
  • Intercepts from standard form: set the other variable to $0$.
  • Parallel lines share a slope; perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$ (flip the fraction and the sign).
  • When the choices are points, plug them in—testing pairs is usually faster than graphing.
Keep the two-variable mindset: the question never asks for the solution—it asks for a solution, a slope, an intercept, or a meaning. Know which one before you compute.
AlgebraLinear Equations in Two Variables: What Counts as a Solution?

What Counts as a Solution?

Rule: The pair $(x, y)$ is a solution exactly when substituting both values makes the equation true—which is exactly when the point $(x, y)$ lies on the line.

Example

Is $(3, 2)$ a solution of $4x - 5y = 2$? Substitute both coordinates:

$$4(3) - 5(2) = 2$$
Replace $x$ with $3$ and $y$ with $2$
$$12 - 10 = 2$$
Multiply
$$2 = 2$$
True — so $(3, 2)$ is a solution
  • $(3, 2)$: $4(3) - 5(2) = 2$
    True statement — the point sits on the line.
  • $(1, 2)$: $4(1) - 5(2) = -6 \neq 2$
    False statement — the point misses the line.
Example

Which of the following ordered pairs $(x, y)$ is a solution to the equation $3x + 2y = 18$?

  1. A)$(2, 6)$
  2. B)$(6, 2)$
  3. C)$(3, 4)$
  4. D)$(4, 2)$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: $3(2) + 2(6) = 6 + 12 = 18$. True—this pair is on the line.
  • Choice B: $3(6) + 2(2) = 22$. Same numbers, swapped order—an ordered pair means $x$ first, $y$ second.
  • Choice C: $3(3) + 2(4) = 17$. Close is not on the line.
  • Choice D: $3(4) + 2(2) = 16$. False statement—not a solution.
Order matters: $(2, 6)$ and $(6, 2)$ are different points. Swapped-coordinate traps appear constantly in this question type.
AlgebraLinear Equations in Two Variables: Two Forms of the Same Line

Two Forms of the Same Line

Any linear equation can wear either costume. Converting between them is one skill: solve for $y$ to reveal the slope, or clear fractions to rebuild the total.

From Standard to Slope-Intercept

Example

What is the slope of the graph of $4x + 2y = 10$ in the $xy$-plane?

$$4x + 2y = 10$$
Given equation
$$2y = -4x + 10$$
Subtract $4x$ from both sides
$$y = -2x + 5$$
Divide both sides by $2$

Now the line reads itself: slope $\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{-2}$, y-intercept $\textcolor{#157f4b}{(0, 5)}$.

Doing that conversion in general produces three shortcuts worth memorizing for $Ax + By = C$:

From $Ax + By = C$ShortcutCheck with $4x + 2y = 10$
Slope$-\frac{A}{B}$$-\frac{4}{2} = -2$
y-intercept (set $x = 0$)$\left(0, \frac{C}{B}\right)$$\frac{10}{2} = 5$
x-intercept (set $y = 0$)$\left(\frac{C}{A}, 0\right)$$\frac{10}{4} = 2.5$

Intercepts Straight from Standard Form

Standard form makes intercepts almost free: zero out one variable and divide.

Example

Find both intercepts of the graph of $3x + 5y = 30$.

$$3x + 5(0) = 30 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 10$$
Set $y = 0$: the x-intercept is $(10, 0)$
$$3(0) + 5y = 30 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 6$$
Set $x = 0$: the y-intercept is $(0, 6)$
Two intercepts are also the fastest way to sketch a standard-form line: mark both, connect the dots.
AlgebraLinear Equations in Two Variables: Building Equations from Context

Building Equations from Context

Standard form is the natural language of combination stories: two things, each with its own per-unit value, adding up to one total.

Value × Quantity+Value × Quantity=Total
Example

A cafe sells a small coffee for $\$4$ and a large coffee for $\$6$. One morning, the cafe earns $\$120$ from coffee sales. If $s$ is the number of small coffees and $l$ the number of large coffees sold, the situation is modeled by:

$$\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{4}s + \textcolor{#0e8a7e}{6}l = \textcolor{#157f4b}{120}$$
4 dollars per small · 6 dollars per large · 120 dollars in total

One equation, many mornings: $(s, l) = (12, 12)$ works, and so does $(30, 0)$—every solution is a possible sales combination. Given one value, the other is forced:

$$4(18) + 6l = 120$$
Suppose $18$ small coffees were sold
$$6l = 48$$
Subtract $72$ from both sides
$$l = 8$$
Divide by $6$ — so $8$ large coffees were sold
Example

At a craft fair, Lena sells bracelets and keychains. The equation $5x + 3y = 90$ models a day when she earned $\$90$, where $x$ is the number of bracelets and $y$ is the number of keychains sold. What is the best interpretation of the number $5$ in this context?

  1. A)Lena sold $5$ bracelets that day
  2. B)Each bracelet was sold for $\$5$
  3. C)Lena earned $\$5$ in total from bracelets
  4. D)Bracelets cost $\$5$ more than keychains
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: The number of bracelets is the variable $x$—not its coefficient. $5$ is what each one contributes.
  • Choice B: $5$ multiplies the number of bracelets, so every bracelet adds $\$5$ to the total—it's the price per bracelet.
  • Choice C: The total earned from bracelets is the whole term $5x$, not the coefficient alone.
  • Choice D: Comparing prices would involve $5 - 3$; the equation says nothing about a difference.
Read coefficients with units: $5$ dollars per bracelet $\times$ $x$ bracelets $=$ dollars. If an interpretation breaks the units, it's wrong.
AlgebraLinear Equations in Two Variables: Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

Slopes control direction—so slopes decide when two lines run alongside each other and when they cross at a right angle.

Parallel: same slope
$$m_1 = m_2$$
Perpendicular: slopes multiply to −1
$$m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$$

Rule: Perpendicular slope = negative reciprocal: flip the fraction and change the sign. The slope $2$ becomes $-\frac{1}{2}$; the slope $-\frac{3}{4}$ becomes $\frac{4}{3}$.

Example

In the $xy$-plane, which equation defines a line that is parallel to the graph of $y = 2x - 7$?

  1. A)$y = 2x + 3$
  2. B)$y = -2x - 7$
  3. C)$y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 3$
  4. D)$y = 7x - 2$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Same slope ($2$), different y-intercept—the definition of parallel.
  • Choice B: Slope $-2$ is the opposite slope, not the same one—this line crosses the original.
  • Choice C: $-\frac{1}{2}$ is the negative reciprocal—that's the perpendicular line, not the parallel one.
  • Choice D: Recycles the numbers $7$ and $2$ in new positions; the slope $7$ matches nothing relevant.
Example

Line $t$ is perpendicular to the graph of $y = 3x - 4$ and passes through the point $(6, 1)$. What is the y-intercept of line $t$?

$$m_t = -\frac{1}{3}$$
Negative reciprocal of $3$
$$1 = -\frac{1}{3}(6) + b$$
Plug the point $(6, 1)$ into $y = m_t x + b$
$$1 = -2 + b$$
Simplify
$$b = 3$$
The y-intercept is $(0, 3)$
The classic perpendicular error is doing only half the job—flipping without negating, or negating without flipping. Check yourself: the two slopes must multiply to $-1$.
AlgebraLinear Equations in Two Variables: Practice

Practice

Name the job first: test a point, find a slope, build an equation, or apply a slope relationship. Commit to an answer before you open the explanation.

Practice
  1. 1.

    Which ordered pair $(x, y)$ is a solution to the equation $5x - 2y = 4$?

    1. A)$(2, 3)$
    2. B)$(3, 2)$
    3. C)$(0, 2)$
    4. D)$(2, -3)$

    The correct answer is A. Substitute each pair until one makes the equation true.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: $5(2) - 2(3) = 10 - 6 = 4$. True—this point is on the line.
    • Choice B: $5(3) - 2(2) = 11$. The swapped-order trap: $x$ comes first in an ordered pair.
    • Choice C: $5(0) - 2(2) = -4$. Right size, wrong sign—$-4 \neq 4$.
    • Choice D: $5(2) - 2(-3) = 10 + 6 = 16$. Subtracting a negative adds—the sign flip works against you here.
  2. 2.

    What is the slope of the graph of $6x + 3y = 12$ in the $xy$-plane?

    1. A)$-2$
    2. B)$2$
    3. C)$-6$
    4. D)$4$

    The correct answer is A. Solve for $y$ (or use the shortcut $-\frac{A}{B}$):

    $$3y = -6x + 12$$
    Subtract $6x$ from both sides
    $$y = -2x + 4$$
    Divide both sides by $3$
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: The coefficient of $x$ after solving for $y$: $-\frac{6}{3} = -2$.
    • Choice B: $2$ drops the sign—moving $6x$ across the equals sign negates it.
    • Choice C: $-6$ reads the $x$-coefficient straight from standard form without dividing by $B = 3$.
    • Choice D: $4$ is the y-intercept, not the slope.
  3. 3.

    A theater sells matinee tickets for $\$9$ each and evening tickets for $\$14$ each. One day, ticket sales totaled $\$252$. If $m$ is the number of matinee tickets and $e$ is the number of evening tickets sold that day, which equation represents this situation?

    1. A)$9m + 14e = 252$
    2. B)$14m + 9e = 252$
    3. C)$m + e = 252$
    4. D)$23(m + e) = 252$

    The correct answer is A. Each ticket type contributes price × quantity, and the contributions add to the day's total.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: $9$ dollars per matinee times $m$, plus $14$ dollars per evening times $e$, equals $252$ dollars. Units check out.
    • Choice B: Swaps the prices: it charges matinee-goers the evening price and vice versa.
    • Choice C: $m + e$ counts tickets, but $252$ is dollars—the units don't match.
    • Choice D: $23(m + e)$ charges every ticket the combined price of both types—no ticket costs $\$23$.
  4. 4.

    In the $xy$-plane, line $t$ is perpendicular to the graph of $y = 2x + 3$ and passes through the point $(4, 1)$. At what point does line $t$ cross the $y$-axis?

    1. A)$(0, 3)$
    2. B)$(0, -7)$
    3. C)$(0, 9)$
    4. D)$(0, -1)$

    The correct answer is A. Perpendicular to slope $2$ means slope $-\frac{1}{2}$—flip and negate—then plug in the point:

    $$m_t = -\frac{1}{2}$$
    Negative reciprocal of $2$
    $$1 = -\frac{1}{2}(4) + b$$
    Use the point $(4, 1)$
    $$b = 3$$
    $1 = -2 + b$ — line $t$ crosses at $(0, 3)$
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Slope $-\frac{1}{2}$ through $(4, 1)$ gives $b = 3$. Check: $2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$. ✓
    • Choice B: $(0, -7)$ keeps the slope $2$ unchanged—that's the parallel setup, not perpendicular.
    • Choice C: $(0, 9)$ uses slope $-2$: negated but never flipped. Half the job.
    • Choice D: $(0, -1)$ uses slope $\frac{1}{2}$: flipped but never negated. The other half.

Things to Remember

  • A solution of a two-variable equation is an ordered pair—and a point on the line. Substitute both coordinates to test it; watch for swapped-order traps.
  • Two forms, one line: $y = mx + b$ shows slope and intercept; $Ax + By = C$ shows totals. Convert by solving for $y$; from standard form, slope $= -\frac{A}{B}$.
  • Intercepts from standard form: set the other variable to $0$ and divide.
  • Combination stories: value × quantity $+$ value × quantity $=$ total. Coefficients are per-unit values—read them with units.
  • Parallel: same slope. Perpendicular: flip and negate—the slopes must multiply to $-1$.
  • When answer choices are points or pairs, plug them in instead of graphing.
