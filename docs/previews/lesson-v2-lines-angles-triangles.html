Lines, Angles, and Triangles

Geometry questions here are angle-chasing: each figure hides a chain of small, certain facts—vertical angles, straight lines, triangle sums—and you follow the chain link by link until the unknown falls out. The full toolkit is short:

FactStatement
Straight lineangles on a line sum to $180°$
Around a pointangles around a point sum to $360°$
Vertical anglesopposite angles at a crossing are equal
Parallel lines + transversalonly two angle values exist, summing to $180°$
Triangle suminterior angles sum to $180°$
Exterior angleequals the sum of the two remote interior angles
Isosceles triangleequal sides face equal base angles
Similar trianglesequal angles; corresponding sides proportional

Strategies

  • Mark everything you deduce on the figure—each new angle unlocks the next.
  • At any crossing: vertical angles match; neighbors sum to $180°$.
  • Parallel lines: decide equal (both acute or both obtuse) vs. supplementary (one of each)—that's the whole classification.
  • In triangles: two known angles always buy the third; tick marks (equal sides) buy equal angles.
  • Similar triangles: match corresponding sides by position across from equal angles, then set up one proportion—in the same order on both sides.
Figures on the SAT are usually to scale unless marked otherwise—use your eyes to check (an angle that looks acute isn't $130°$), but use the chain of facts to prove.
Geometry and TrigonometryLines, Angles, and Triangles: Angles at Lines and Points

Angles at Lines and Points

Example
$$x = 130$$
Vertical angles — opposite openings are equal
$$y = 180 - 130 = 50$$
Linear pair — neighbors on a straight line sum to $180°$

Around a full point, the angles complete a circle: they sum to $\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{360°}$. Keep the two totals straight—line $180°$, point $360°$—because the test builds traps from exactly that mix-up:

Example

Three angles measuring $x°$, $2x°$, and $60°$ together form a complete rotation around a point. What is the value of $x$?

  1. A)$100$
  2. B)$40$
  3. C)$60$
  4. D)$200$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Around a point: $x + 2x + 60 = 360$, so $3x = 300$ and $x = 100$.
  • Choice B: $40$ solves $3x + 60 = 180$—the straight line total, but these angles complete a full rotation.
  • Choice C: $60$ just copies the given angle; nothing forces $x$ to match it.
  • Choice D: $200$ is $2x$—the middle angle's measure, not $x$.
Angle-chasing bookkeeping: every time you compute an angle, write it on the figure immediately. The next fact is usually waiting right next to it.
Geometry and TrigonometryLines, Angles, and Triangles: Parallel Lines & Transversals

Parallel Lines & Transversals

A transversal crossing parallel lines creates eight angles—but only two different values. Every acute angle equals every other acute; every obtuse equals every obtuse; and any acute–obtuse pair sums to $180°$.

$$a = 65$$
Alternate interior angles (the “Z” pattern) — both acute, so equal
$$b = 180 - 65 = 115$$
Same-side interior angles — one acute, one obtuse, so supplementary
Skip the vocabulary quiz (corresponding, alternate, co-interior…) and ask one question: do the two angles look the same type, or different? Same type → equal. Different → sum to $180°$. That shortcut answers every parallel-line question—when the lines are parallel.
Example

In the figure, lines $\ell$ and $m$ are parallel. What is the value of $x$?

  1. A)$40$
  2. B)$25$
  3. C)$52.5$
  4. D)$105$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Both angles are the same type (the “Z” pair), so they're equal: $2x + 25 = 105$, giving $2x = 80$ and $x = 40$.
  • Choice B: $x = 25$ comes from $2x + 25 = 75$—treating the pair as supplementary. They're the same type, so they match, not sum.
  • Choice C: $x = 52.5$ halves the $105$ without ever subtracting the $25$.
  • Choice D: $x = 105$ confuses the angle's measure with $x$—the variable inside the expression.
Geometry and TrigonometryLines, Angles, and Triangles: Triangle Angles

Triangle Angles

Rule: The interior angles of every triangle sum to $180°$. Two known angles always determine the third.

Example
$$x = 180 - 52 - 63 = 65$$
Triangle sum

The Exterior Angle Shortcut

Extend one side and the new exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles—the triangle sum and the straight line, fused into one step:

Example
$$x + 45 = 115$$
Exterior $=$ sum of the two remote interiors
$$x = 70$$
One subtraction — no need to find the third angle first

Isosceles: Tick Marks Buy Angles

Equal sides (shown by tick marks) sit opposite equal angles. Given the apex angle, the two base angles split what's left of $180°$ evenly.

Example

In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$ and the measure of angle $A$ is $48°$. What is the measure of angle $B$?

  1. A)$66°$
  2. B)$48°$
  3. C)$132°$
  4. D)$42°$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: The equal sides $AB$ and $AC$ face equal angles at $B$ and $C$: each is $\frac{180 - 48}{2} = 66°$.
  • Choice B: $48°$ copies angle $A$—but $A$ is the apex between the equal sides, not one of the matched pair.
  • Choice C: $132°$ is $180 - 48$ unhalved—the two base angles' combined share, not one angle.
  • Choice D: $42°$ computes $90 - 48$: a complement, which nothing in this triangle asks for.
Geometry and TrigonometryLines, Angles, and Triangles: Similar Triangles

Similar Triangles

Similar triangles are the same shape at different sizes: all angles equal, all sides scaled by the same factor. Two matching angles are enough to prove it (AA)—the third comes free from the triangle sum.

Example
$$k = \frac{9}{6} = 1.5$$
Scale factor from one matched pair of sides
$$8 \times 1.5 = 12, \qquad 10 \times 1.5 = 15$$
Every other side scales by the same $k$

The Nested Version

The SAT's favorite similar-triangle setup hides one triangle inside another: a segment drawn parallel to a side creates a smaller triangle similar to the whole thing.

Example
$$AB = 4 + 2 = 6$$
The big triangle's side is the whole length $AD + DB$
$$\frac{DE}{BC} = \frac{AD}{AB} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{6}{x} = \frac{4}{6}$$
Corresponding sides, same order: small over big
$$x = 9$$
Cross-multiply: $4x = 36$
The classic nested-triangle error: using $DB = 2$ in the ratio. The big triangle's side runs from $A$ all the way to $B$—compare whole side to whole side ($AD$ with $AB$), never the leftover piece.
Example

A person $6$ feet tall casts a shadow $4$ feet long. At the same time, a nearby tree casts a shadow $18$ feet long. What is the height, in feet, of the tree?

  1. A)$27$
  2. B)$12$
  3. C)$16$
  4. D)$72$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Sunlight makes two similar right triangles: $\frac{\text{height}}{\text{shadow}} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5$ for both, so the tree is $18 \times 1.5 = 27$ feet.
  • Choice B: $12$ uses the ratio upside down ($18 \times \frac{4}{6}$)—that would make the tree shorter relative to its shadow than the person.
  • Choice C: $16$ reasons additively (“the person's height is $2$ more than the shadow”)—similarity scales by multiplication, never by a fixed difference.
  • Choice D: $72$ multiplies $18 \times 4$—two lengths multiplied gives an area-like number, not a height.
Geometry and TrigonometryLines, Angles, and Triangles: Practice

Practice

Chase the chain: mark what you know, apply one fact at a time—and in similarity questions, check the ratio's order twice.

Practice
  1. 1.

    Two lines intersect, and a pair of vertical angles measures $(3x - 10)°$ and $(2x + 15)°$. What is the value of $x$?

    1. A)$25$
    2. B)$35$
    3. C)$5$
    4. D)$65$

    The correct answer is A. Vertical angles are equal:

    $$3x - 10 = 2x + 15$$
    Set the vertical pair equal
    $$x = 25$$
    Subtract $2x$, add $10$ — each angle is $65°$
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Check: $3(25) - 10 = 65$ and $2(25) + 15 = 65$. ✓
    • Choice B: $35$ solves $5x + 5 = 180$—treating the pair as a linear pair. Vertical angles match; neighbors sum.
    • Choice C: $5$ comes from botching the collection step ($x = 15 - 10$).
    • Choice D: $65$ is each angle's measure—the question asks for $x$.
  2. 2.

    Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two same-side interior angles measure $(5x)°$ and $(4x)°$. What is the value of $x$?

    1. A)$20$
    2. B)$36$
    3. C)$45$
    4. D)$10$

    The correct answer is A. Same-side interior angles are one acute, one obtuse—supplementary:

    $$5x + 4x = 180$$
    Different types → sum to $180°$
    $$x = 20$$
    $9x = 180$ — the angles are $100°$ and $80°$
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: $100 + 80 = 180$. ✓
    • Choice B: $36$ sets $5x = 180$ alone—as if one angle filled the whole straight line.
    • Choice C: $45$ does the same with $4x = 180$.
    • Choice D: $10$ solves $9x = 90$—a right angle where the fact demands $180°$.
  3. 3.

    In the figure, what is the value of $x$?

    1. A)$68$
    2. B)$54$
    3. C)$32$
    4. D)$63$

    The correct answer is A. Exterior angle theorem: $x + 58 = 126$, so $x = 68$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Check the long way: the interior angle beside $126°$ is $54°$, and $68 + 58 + 54 = 180$. ✓
    • Choice B: $54$ is $180 - 126$: the adjacent interior angle, not the remote $x$.
    • Choice C: $32$ computes $90 - 58$—no right angle appears anywhere in this figure.
    • Choice D: $63$ halves the $126$, as if the two remote angles had to be equal. Nothing says they are.
  4. 4.

    In triangle $ABC$, point $D$ lies on side $\overline{AB}$ and point $E$ lies on side $\overline{AC}$ such that $\overline{DE} \parallel \overline{BC}$. If $AD = 6$, $AB = 9$, and $DE = 10$, what is the length of $\overline{BC}$?

    1. A)$15$
    2. B)$6.7$
    3. C)$13$
    4. D)$30$

    The correct answer is A. $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$ because $\overline{DE} \parallel \overline{BC}$:

    $$\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$$
    Small triangle to big triangle — whole sides, both times
    $$\frac{DE}{BC} = \frac{2}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; BC = \frac{3}{2}(10) = 15$$
    Same ratio for every pair of corresponding sides
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: The big triangle is $\frac{3}{2}$ the size, so its base is $15$. ✓
    • Choice B: $6.7 \approx 10 \times \frac{6}{9}$ shrinks instead of enlarging—$BC$ belongs to the bigger triangle.
    • Choice C: $13$ adds the difference $9 - 6 = 3$ to $10$—additive thinking where similarity multiplies.
    • Choice D: $30$ scales by $DB = 3$ as a factor—the leftover piece $DB$ isn't a side of either similar triangle.

Things to Remember

  • Line $= 180°$, point $= 360°$, vertical angles equal. Write every deduced angle on the figure.
  • Parallel lines + transversal: only two values exist—same type → equal, different type → supplementary.
  • Triangle sum $180°$; the exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interiors—one step instead of two.
  • Isosceles: equal sides face equal angles; base angles $= \frac{180 - \text{apex}}{2}$.
  • Similar triangles (AA): sides scale by one factor $k$. Set ratios in matching order, and in nested figures compare whole side to whole side—never the leftover piece.
  • Similarity multiplies, never adds—an answer built from a difference of sides is a trap.
