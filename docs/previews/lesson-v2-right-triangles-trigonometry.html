Right Triangles and Trigonometry

Everything in this skill happens inside a right triangle, with two toolkits: the Pythagorean theorem relates the three sides, and the trig ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) connect sides to angles. Knowing which toolkit a question wants is half the solve.

Strategies

  • The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle—always the longest, always $c$.
  • Opposite and adjacent are relative to the chosen angle. Re-label them every time the angle changes.
  • Memorize the triples—$3$-$4$-$5$, $5$-$12$-$13$, $7$-$24$-$25$, $8$-$15$-$17$ and their multiples—to skip the arithmetic entirely.
  • Special triangles: $45$-$45$-$90$ → $x, x, x\sqrt{2}$; $30$-$60$-$90$ → $x, x\sqrt{3}, 2x$ (short leg opposite $30°$).
  • Cofunction identity: $\sin(x°) = \cos(90° - x°)$—the acute angles of a right triangle are complements.
  • Similar right triangles have identical trig ratios—size never changes sine.
Draw and label before computing: mark the right angle, star the angle in play, then write O, A, H on the three sides. Thirty seconds of labeling prevents every inverted ratio.
Geometry and TrigonometryRight Triangles and Trigonometry: The Pythagorean Theorem

The Pythagorean Theorem

Example
$$c^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 100$$
Legs squared and added
$$c = 10$$
Square root — a scaled $3$-$4$-$5$ triple

Finding a Leg: Subtract

Example

A right triangle has a hypotenuse of $13$ and one leg of $5$. What is the other leg?

$$b^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 144$$
The hypotenuse goes alone on its side — subtract the known leg
$$b = 12$$
The $5$-$12$-$13$ triple, confirmed
Example

In a right triangle, the hypotenuse has length $15$ and one leg has length $9$. What is the length of the other leg?

  1. A)$12$
  2. B)$\sqrt{306} \approx 17.5$
  3. C)$6$
  4. D)$144$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: $\sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ — a $3$-$4$-$5$ triple scaled by $3$.
  • Choice B: $\sqrt{225 + 81}$ adds—but the $15$ is the hypotenuse, so it stands alone. Adding also produces a side longer than the hypotenuse: impossible.
  • Choice C: $15 - 9 = 6$ subtracts the lengths instead of their squares.
  • Choice D: $144$ is $b^2$—the square root never happened.
Triples to know on sight: $3$-$4$-$5$, $5$-$12$-$13$, $7$-$24$-$25$, $8$-$15$-$17$—and every multiple ($6$-$8$-$10$, $9$-$12$-$15$, $10$-$24$-$26$…). Spotting one converts a calculation into a glance.
Geometry and TrigonometryRight Triangles and Trigonometry: Special Right Triangles

Special Right Triangles

Two angle patterns come with fixed side ratios (both are on the reference sheet, both are worth knowing cold):

$45°$-$45°$-$90°$: $x,\; x,\; x\sqrt{2}$
$30°$-$60°$-$90°$: $x,\; x\sqrt{3},\; 2x$

Rule: Anchor on the short leg $x$ (opposite the $30°$): hypotenuse $= 2x$, long leg $= x\sqrt{3}$. In the $45$er, the legs match and the hypotenuse picks up the $\sqrt{2}$.

Example

A $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle has a hypotenuse of length $12$. Find the legs.

$$x = \frac{12}{2} = 6$$
Short leg — half the hypotenuse
$$x\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3} \approx 10.4$$
Long leg — short leg times $\sqrt{3}$
Example

In a $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle, the longer leg has length $9$. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

  1. A)$6\sqrt{3}$
  2. B)$18$
  3. C)$9\sqrt{3}$
  4. D)$4.5$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Long leg $= x\sqrt{3} = 9$ gives $x = \frac{9}{\sqrt{3}} = 3\sqrt{3}$; hypotenuse $= 2x = 6\sqrt{3} \approx 10.4$. Sanity: just longer than the $9$ leg. ✓
  • Choice B: $18$ doubles the $9$—but doubling applies to the short leg, and $9$ is the long one.
  • Choice C: $9\sqrt{3} \approx 15.6$ multiplies by $\sqrt{3}$ a second time—that converts short to long, not long to hypotenuse.
  • Choice D: $4.5$ halves—the hypotenuse must be the longest side, not shorter than a leg.
Before applying a pattern, ask which side is the $9$? Short leg, long leg, and hypotenuse each convert differently—the wrong choices above are the other two conversions.
Geometry and TrigonometryRight Triangles and Trigonometry: SOH-CAH-TOA

SOH-CAH-TOA

Trig ratios name the three possible side-pairs of a right triangle, as seen from one acute angle $\theta$:

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\textcolor{#c0392f}{\text{opp}}}{\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{\text{hyp}}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\textcolor{#0e8a7e}{\text{adj}}}{\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{\text{hyp}}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\textcolor{#c0392f}{\text{opp}}}{\textcolor{#0e8a7e}{\text{adj}}}$$
SOH · CAH · TOA
Example

In the triangle shown, what is the value of $\tan A$?

  1. A)$\frac{3}{4}$
  2. B)$\frac{4}{3}$
  3. C)$\frac{3}{5}$
  4. D)$\frac{4}{5}$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: From $A$: opposite $= BC = 6$, adjacent $= AC = 8$. $\tan A = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$.
  • Choice B: $\frac{8}{6}$ is $\tan B$—the ratio as seen from the other acute angle. Opposite and adjacent swap when the angle does.
  • Choice C: $\frac{6}{10}$ is $\sin A$—right sides, wrong ratio.
  • Choice D: $\frac{8}{10}$ is $\cos A$.

Using a Ratio to Find a Side

Example

In a right triangle, $\cos B = \frac{12}{13}$ and the hypotenuse has length $26$. What is the length of the side adjacent to angle $B$?

$$\frac{\text{adj}}{26} = \frac{12}{13}$$
$\cos = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, with the knowns dropped in
$$\text{adj} = 26 \times \frac{12}{13} = 24$$
The triangle is $5$-$12$-$13$ scaled by $2$
A given ratio like $\cos B = \frac{12}{13}$ is a similar-triangle statement: the real triangle is the $12$-$13$ reference triangle scaled by some factor. Find the factor, scale every side.
Geometry and TrigonometryRight Triangles and Trigonometry: The Complement Identity

The Complement Identity

A right triangle's two acute angles sum to $90°$—and one angle's opposite side is the other's adjacent. Swapping viewpoints swaps sine and cosine:

$$\sin(x°) = \cos(90° - x°)$$
Sine of an angle $=$ cosine of its complement — and vice versa

So $\sin 38° = \cos 52°$, $\cos 71° = \sin 19°$—no calculator, just $90°$ minus the angle. The SAT tests this identity directly and often:

Example

In a right triangle, $\sin(x°) = \cos(23°)$. If $0 < x < 90$, what is the value of $x$?

  1. A)$67$
  2. B)$23$
  3. C)$157$
  4. D)$46$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Sine equals the cosine of the complement: $x = 90 - 23 = 67$.
  • Choice B: $23$ would need $\sin$ and $\cos$ of the same angle to match—only true at $45°$.
  • Choice C: $157$ uses $180 - 23$: supplements belong to straight lines, not to this identity.
  • Choice D: $46$ doubles the $23$—no identity does that.

Similar Triangles, Identical Ratios

Trig ratios depend only on the triangle's angles—its shape. All right triangles containing a $35°$ angle are similar, so $\sin 35°$ is the same number in every one of them, from a stamp to a stadium:

  • $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle: $\sin\theta = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6$. Scaled to $6$-$8$-$10$: $\sin\theta = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6$
    Both sides of the ratio scale by the same factor, which cancels. Size in, size out.
This is why “triangle $DEF$ is similar to triangle $ABC$…” questions can ask for $\sin F$ using $ABC$'s sides: corresponding angles have identical trig values. Ratios travel; lengths don't.
Geometry and TrigonometryRight Triangles and Trigonometry: Practice

Practice

Label first—right angle, chosen angle, O/A/H—then pick the toolkit: Pythagorean, special pattern, or ratio.

Practice
  1. 1.

    A $25$-foot ladder leans against a vertical wall, with its base $7$ feet from the wall on level ground. How high up the wall, in feet, does the ladder reach?

    1. A)$24$
    2. B)$26$
    3. C)$18$
    4. D)$32$

    The correct answer is A. The ladder is the hypotenuse:

    $$h^2 = 25^2 - 7^2 = 576$$
    Hypotenuse alone; subtract the known leg
    $$h = 24$$
    The $7$-$24$-$25$ triple
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Check: $49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$. ✓
    • Choice B: $\sqrt{625 + 49} \approx 26$ adds—and reaches higher than the ladder is long, which no wall allows.
    • Choice C: $18 = 25 - 7$ subtracts lengths, not squares.
    • Choice D: $32 = 25 + 7$ adds lengths—the ladder isn't a folding ruler.
  2. 2.

    The hypotenuse of a $45°$-$45°$-$90°$ triangle has length $8$. What is the length of each leg?

    1. A)$4\sqrt{2}$
    2. B)$8\sqrt{2}$
    3. C)$4$
    4. D)$16$

    The correct answer is A. Hypotenuse $= x\sqrt{2} = 8$, so $x = \frac{8}{\sqrt{2}} = 4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.7$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Sanity: legs ($\approx 5.7$) shorter than the hypotenuse ($8$). ✓
    • Choice B: $8\sqrt{2}$ multiplies instead of divides—that's the hypotenuse of a triangle whose leg is $8$, the conversion run backward.
    • Choice C: $4$ halves the hypotenuse—that's the $30$-$60$-$90$ move, wrong triangle.
    • Choice D: $16$ doubles it—no pattern in either special triangle does this to a hypotenuse.
  3. 3.

    In right triangle $PQR$, $\sin R = \frac{5}{13}$. If the hypotenuse has length $39$, what is the length of the side opposite angle $R$?

    1. A)$15$
    2. B)$36$
    3. C)$5$
    4. D)$101.4$

    The correct answer is A. Sine is opposite over hypotenuse:

    $$\frac{\text{opp}}{39} = \frac{5}{13}$$
    SOH with the knowns in place
    $$\text{opp} = 39 \times \frac{5}{13} = 15$$
    The $5$-$12$-$13$ reference triangle scaled by $3$
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Scale factor $3$: sides $15$-$36$-$39$. ✓
    • Choice B: $36$ is the adjacent side ($12 \times 3$)—the cosine's side, not the sine's.
    • Choice C: $5$ reads the ratio's numerator as a length, ignoring the scale factor.
    • Choice D: $101.4 = 39 \times \frac{13}{5}$—the ratio flipped upside down, producing a side longer than the hypotenuse.
  4. 4.

    In a right triangle, one acute angle measures $x°$ and $\cos(x°) = \sin(19°)$. What is the value of $x$?

    1. A)$71$
    2. B)$19$
    3. C)$161$
    4. D)$38$

    The correct answer is A. Cosine of an angle equals sine of its complement: $x = 90 - 19 = 71$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: $\cos 71° = \sin 19°$—the identity, both directions. ✓
    • Choice B: $19$ assumes $\sin$ and $\cos$ agree at the same angle—only at $45°$.
    • Choice C: $161 = 180 - 19$ reaches for supplements—and isn't even an acute angle.
    • Choice D: $38 = 2 \times 19$—doubling isn't part of any trig identity here.

Things to Remember

  • $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ with $c$ the hypotenuse (opposite the right angle, always longest). Finding a leg? The hypotenuse stands alone—subtract.
  • Triples on sight: $3$-$4$-$5$, $5$-$12$-$13$, $7$-$24$-$25$, $8$-$15$-$17$, and all their multiples.
  • $45$-$45$-$90$: $x, x, x\sqrt{2}$. $30$-$60$-$90$: $x, x\sqrt{3}, 2x$—anchor on the short leg, opposite the $30°$. Identify which side you're given before converting.
  • SOH-CAH-TOA, with opposite/adjacent defined by the chosen angle—they swap when the angle does.
  • $\sin(x°) = \cos(90° - x°)$: sine and cosine trade places across complements.
  • Similar right triangles share identical trig ratios—a given ratio is a reference triangle; find the scale factor and every side follows.
