Circles

Circle questions live in two worlds. In the geometry world, arcs and sectors are just fractions of one circle. In the coordinate world, a circle is an equation whose center and radius you read off—or reconstruct. Both worlds run on a short set of facts:

FactFormula
Circumference / area$C = 2\pi r$ · $A = \pi r^2$
Fraction cut by a central angle$\dfrac{\text{angle}}{360°}$
Arc length / sector areafraction $\times\, C$ · fraction $\times\, A$
Circle equation$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, center $(h, k)$
Degrees ↔ radians$180° = \pi$ radians
Tangent lineperpendicular to the radius at the touch point

Strategies

  • Everything proportional starts with the fraction $\frac{\text{central angle}}{360°}$—then multiply by the whole circumference or the whole area.
  • Reading the equation: the signs flip ($x - 3$ means $h = +3$; $y + 2$ means $k = -2$), and the right side is $r^2$—take the square root.
  • Messy equation? Complete the square: half the coefficient, square it, add to both sides.
  • Tangent = right angle. A tangent meeting a radius builds a right triangle—Pythagorean toolkit applies.
  • Radians: multiply degrees by $\frac{\pi}{180}$.
Two slips cause most circle mistakes: forgetting the sign flip in the equation, and reporting $r^2$ as the radius. Check both, every time.
Geometry and TrigonometryCircles: Arcs and Sectors: Fractions of a Circle

Arcs and Sectors: Fractions of a Circle

Rule: A central angle of $\theta$ degrees cuts off $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of the circle—of its circumference (arc length) and of its area (sector area) alike.

Example
Arc length
$$C = 2\pi(6) = 12\pi$$
Whole circumference
$$\frac{1}{6} \times 12\pi = 2\pi \approx 6.3$$
The $60°$ share
Sector area
$$A = \pi(6)^2 = 36\pi$$
Whole area
$$\frac{1}{6} \times 36\pi = 6\pi \approx 18.8$$
Same fraction, applied to area
Example

A circle has a radius of $9$. What is the area of a sector with a central angle of $120°$?

  1. A)$27\pi$
  2. B)$81\pi$
  3. C)$6\pi$
  4. D)$54\pi$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Fraction $\frac{120}{360} = \frac{1}{3}$; whole area $81\pi$; sector $= \frac{81\pi}{3} = 27\pi$.
  • Choice B: $81\pi$ is the entire circle—the fraction never got applied.
  • Choice C: $6\pi$ is the arc length ($\frac{1}{3}$ of $18\pi$)—right fraction, wrong measurement.
  • Choice D: $54\pi$ uses $\frac{2}{3}$—the fraction of the circle outside the sector.
Arc length and sector area use the same fraction but different wholes—circumference ($2\pi r$) versus area ($\pi r^2$). Check which one the question asks for before multiplying.
Geometry and TrigonometryCircles: The Circle Equation

The Circle Equation

$$(x - \textcolor{#0f6cbd}{h})^2 + (y - \textcolor{#0f6cbd}{k})^2 = \textcolor{#c0392f}{r}^2$$
Center $(\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{h}, \textcolor{#0f6cbd}{k})$ · radius $\textcolor{#c0392f}{r}$ — every point at distance $r$ from the center
Example

What are the center and radius of the circle $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$?

$$h = 3, \quad k = -2$$
Signs flip: $(y + 2)$ is $(y - (-2))$
$$r = \sqrt{25} = 5$$
The right side is $r^2$ — root it
Example

Which of the following are the center and radius of the circle with equation $x^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 49$?

  1. A)Center $(0, 4)$; radius $7$
  2. B)Center $(0, -4)$; radius $7$
  3. C)Center $(0, 4)$; radius $49$
  4. D)Center $(0, -4)$; radius $49$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: $x^2$ is $(x - 0)^2$, so $h = 0$; $(y - 4)$ gives $k = +4$; and $r = \sqrt{49} = 7$.
  • Choice B: Flips the sign the wrong way: $(y - 4)$ means the center is at $+4$.
  • Choice C: Right center, but $49$ is $r^2$—the radius is its square root.
  • Choice D: Both classic slips at once.
Quick uses of the equation: a point is on the circle if it satisfies the equation, inside if the left side comes out smaller than $r^2$, outside if larger.
Geometry and TrigonometryCircles: Completing the Square

Completing the Square

Expanded circle equations hide the center and radius: $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$ is the page-before's circle in disguise. Completing the square rebuilds the two perfect squares:

Rule: For $x^2 + bx$: take half of $b$, square it, add it to both sides. The square is then $(x + \frac{b}{2})^2$.

Example

Find the center and radius of the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$.

$$(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 12$$
Group by variable; move the constant across
$$(x^2 - 6x + \textcolor{#157f4b}{9}) + (y^2 + 4y + \textcolor{#157f4b}{4}) = 12 + \textcolor{#157f4b}{9} + \textcolor{#157f4b}{4}$$
Half of $-6$ is $-3$, squared: $9$. Half of $4$ is $2$, squared: $4$. Add to both sides
$$(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$$
Center $(3, -2)$, radius $5$ — the disguise removed
Example

In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $x^2 + y^2 + 8y = 33$ is a circle. What is the radius of the circle?

  1. A)$7$
  2. B)$\sqrt{33}$
  3. C)$49$
  4. D)$4$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Complete the square in $y$: add $\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2 = 16$ to both sides, giving $x^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 49$, so $r = 7$.
  • Choice B: $\sqrt{33}$ takes the root of the right side before completing the square—the $33$ isn't $r^2$ yet.
  • Choice C: $49$ is $r^2$—one square root short.
  • Choice D: $4$ is half the $y$-coefficient—it locates the center ($k = -4$), not the radius.
Whatever you add inside the parentheses must be added to the other side too—the equation stays balanced or the radius comes out wrong.
Geometry and TrigonometryCircles: Radians & Tangent Lines

Radians & Tangent Lines

Radians: Another Unit for Angles

Radians measure angles by arc length: a full circle is $2\pi$ radians, so $\textcolor{#0f6cbd}{180° = \pi \text{ radians}}$. Converting is one multiplication:

Degrees$30°$$45°$$60°$$90°$$180°$$360°$
Radians$\frac{\pi}{6}$$\frac{\pi}{4}$$\frac{\pi}{3}$$\frac{\pi}{2}$$\pi$$2\pi$
Example

Convert $150°$ to radians.

$$150° \times \frac{\pi}{180°} = \frac{150\pi}{180}$$
Multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$ — degrees cancel
$$= \frac{5\pi}{6}$$
Reduce the fraction

Tangents Build Right Triangles

A tangent line touches the circle at exactly one point—and the radius drawn to that point is perpendicular to it. That right angle is an invitation: center, touch point, and any external point form a right triangle.

Example
$$x^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 144$$
Pythagorean theorem — $OP$ is the hypotenuse
$$x = 12$$
The $5$-$12$-$13$ triple, one lesson later
Example

Which of the following is the measure $240°$ expressed in radians?

  1. A)$\frac{4\pi}{3}$
  2. B)$\frac{3\pi}{4}$
  3. C)$\frac{2\pi}{3}$
  4. D)$\frac{5\pi}{6}$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: $240 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{240\pi}{180} = \frac{4\pi}{3}$. Sanity: $240°$ is more than half a turn, and $\frac{4\pi}{3} > \pi$. ✓
  • Choice B: $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ flips the reduced fraction—and equals $135°$, less than half a turn.
  • Choice C: $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ is $120°$—half of the target angle.
  • Choice D: $\frac{5\pi}{6}$ is $150°$—a different conversion entirely.
Radian sanity check: compare with $\pi$. More than a half-turn ($> 180°$) must convert to more than $\pi$; less converts to less. It filters wrong choices instantly.
Geometry and TrigonometryCircles: Practice

Practice

Fraction first for arcs and sectors; sign-flip and square-root checks for equations; and when a tangent appears, look for the right triangle.

Practice
  1. 1.

    A circle has radius $12$. What is the area of a sector with a central angle of $90°$?

    1. A)$36\pi$
    2. B)$144\pi$
    3. C)$6\pi$
    4. D)$12\pi$

    The correct answer is A. The fraction is $\frac{90}{360} = \frac{1}{4}$, and the whole area is $\pi(12)^2 = 144\pi$; the sector is $\frac{144\pi}{4} = 36\pi$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: A quarter of the circle. ✓
    • Choice B: $144\pi$ is the whole circle—no quarter taken.
    • Choice C: $6\pi$ is the arc length: $\frac{1}{4}$ of the circumference $24\pi$. Right fraction, wrong whole.
    • Choice D: $12\pi$ is half the circumference—a length, where the question asks for an area.
  2. 2.

    The equation $(x + 5)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 36$ is graphed in the $xy$-plane. What are the center and radius of the circle?

    1. A)Center $(-5, 1)$; radius $6$
    2. B)Center $(5, -1)$; radius $6$
    3. C)Center $(-5, 1)$; radius $36$
    4. D)Center $(5, -1)$; radius $36$

    The correct answer is A. $(x + 5)$ is $(x - (-5))$, so $h = -5$; $(y - 1)$ gives $k = 1$; and $r = \sqrt{36} = 6$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Both signs flipped, root taken. ✓
    • Choice B: Reads the signs literally—the equation's $+5$ means the center sits at $-5$.
    • Choice C: Center right; $36$ is $r^2$, not $r$.
    • Choice D: Both slips together.
  3. 3.

    The graph of $x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 6y + 18 = 0$ in the $xy$-plane is a circle. What is the radius of the circle?

    1. A)$4$
    2. B)$16$
    3. C)$\sqrt{34}$
    4. D)$5$

    The correct answer is A. Complete both squares:

    $$(x^2 - 10x + \textcolor{#157f4b}{25}) + (y^2 + 6y + \textcolor{#157f4b}{9}) = -18 + \textcolor{#157f4b}{25} + \textcolor{#157f4b}{9}$$
    Halve, square, add to both sides
    $$(x - 5)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16$$
    Center $(5, -3)$
    $$r = \sqrt{16} = 4$$
    Root the right side
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Both squares completed, constant moved, root taken. ✓
    • Choice B: $16$ is $r^2$.
    • Choice C: $\sqrt{34}$ adds the $25$ and $9$ but forgets the equation already carried a $+18$—the constant must move across with its sign.
    • Choice D: $5$ is half the $x$-coefficient—the center's $x$-coordinate, not the radius.
  4. 4.

    A circle has center $O$ and radius $8$. Point $P$ lies outside the circle with $OP = 17$, and a line through $P$ is tangent to the circle at point $T$. What is the length of $\overline{PT}$?

    1. A)$15$
    2. B)$9$
    3. C)$25$
    4. D)$\sqrt{353} \approx 18.8$

    The correct answer is A. The radius $\overline{OT}$ is perpendicular to the tangent, so triangle $OTP$ is right-angled at $T$:

    $$PT^2 = 17^2 - 8^2 = 225$$
    $OP$ is the hypotenuse; the radius $8$ is a leg
    $$PT = 15$$
    The $8$-$15$-$17$ triple
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Tangent ⊥ radius → Pythagorean theorem. ✓
    • Choice B: $9 = 17 - 8$ subtracts lengths instead of squares.
    • Choice C: $25 = 17 + 8$ adds them—no triangle side works that way.
    • Choice D: $\sqrt{289 + 64}$ adds the squares—but $OP$ is the hypotenuse, so its square stands alone.

Things to Remember

  • Arc length and sector area $=$ $\frac{\text{central angle}}{360°}$ times the whole circumference ($2\pi r$) or the whole area ($\pi r^2$)—same fraction, different wholes.
  • Circle equation $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$: signs flip for the center, and the right side is $r^2$—root it.
  • Completing the square: half the coefficient, square it, add to both sides—then read center and radius from the rebuilt form.
  • A point is on/inside/outside the circle when plugging it in gives exactly/less than/more than $r^2$.
  • Tangent ⊥ radius at the touch point—expect a right triangle and reach for the Pythagorean toolkit.
  • Radians: degrees $\times \frac{\pi}{180}$; sanity-check against $\pi$ (a half-turn).
