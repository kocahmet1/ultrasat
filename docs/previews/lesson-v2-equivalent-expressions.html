Equivalent Expressions

Two expressions are equivalent when they're equal for every value of the variable—the same object written two ways. These questions ask you to expand, factor, simplify, or rewrite: “Which expression is equivalent to…?” No solving, just rewriting without changing the value.

Strategies

  • Combine only like terms—same variable and same exponent. $3x^2$ and $5x$ don't mix.
  • Distribute completely. Subtracting a polynomial flips the sign of every term inside.
  • Know the three special products cold: $(a+b)^2$, $(a-b)^2$, and $(a+b)(a-b)$.
  • Exponent rules: multiplying powers adds exponents; a power of a power multiplies them; negative exponents flip to the denominator.
  • Cancel factors, never terms. Fractions simplify only after both top and bottom are factored.
  • Test with a number. Equivalent means equal for every $x$—so plug in one easy value and eliminate any choice that disagrees.
The number test is this skill's superpower. Plug $x = 2$ into the original and into each choice—any mismatch kills that choice instantly. (Avoid $x = 0$ and $x = 1$: too many wrong choices accidentally agree there.)
Advanced MathEquivalent Expressions: Add, Subtract & Multiply Polynomials

Add, Subtract & Multiply Polynomials

Rule: Like terms have the same variable raised to the same power. Adding polynomials = combining like terms; nothing else merges.

Example

Which expression is equivalent to $(3x^2 + 5x - 2) + (x^2 - 8x + 7)$?

$$(3x^2 + x^2) + (5x - 8x) + (-2 + 7)$$
Group like terms by power
$$4x^2 - 3x + 5$$
Combine each group

Subtraction Flips Every Sign

Example

Which expression is equivalent to $(5x^2 - 3x + 4) - (2x^2 + x - 6)$?

$$5x^2 - 3x + 4 - 2x^2 - x + 6$$
Distribute the minus to all three terms
$$3x^2 - 4x + 10$$
Combine like terms
  • $3x^2 - 4x + 10$
    The $-6$ became $+6$: the minus reached the last term too.
  • $3x^2 - 4x - 2$
    Flipped $2x^2$ and $x$ but left $-6$ alone — the most common subtraction error. Test $x = 2$: the original gives $14$, this gives $2$.

Multiply Binomials: Every Term Meets Every Term

Example

Which expression is equivalent to $(2x + 3)(x - 5)$?

$$2x \cdot x + 2x \cdot (-5) + 3 \cdot x + 3 \cdot (-5)$$
Four products: First, Outer, Inner, Last
$$2x^2 - 10x + 3x - 15$$
Multiply each pair
$$2x^2 - 7x - 15$$
Combine the middle terms

Number check with $x = 2$: original $(7)(-3) = -21$; result $8 - 14 - 15 = -21$. ✓

Matching-coefficient questions hide here: if $(2x + 3)(x - 5) = ax^2 + bx + c$ for all $x$, then $a = 2$, $b = -7$, $c = -15$—expand, then read the coefficients.
Advanced MathEquivalent Expressions: Special Products & Factoring

Special Products & Factoring

Three multiplication patterns appear so often that the SAT assumes you know them by sight—forward (expanding) and backward (factoring):

PatternExpansionName
$(a + b)^2$$a^2 + 2ab + b^2$square of a sum
$(a - b)^2$$a^2 - 2ab + b^2$square of a difference
$(a + b)(a - b)$$a^2 - b^2$difference of squares
$(a + b)^2$ is not $a^2 + b^2$. The middle term $2ab$ is exactly what the test's favorite wrong answer leaves out.
Example

Which expression is equivalent to $(3x - 4)^2$?

$$(3x)^2 - 2(3x)(4) + 4^2$$
Square of a difference: $a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
$$9x^2 - 24x + 16$$
Simplify each piece

Factoring: Running the Patterns Backward

$$6x^2 + 15x = 3x(2x + 5)$$
GCF first, always — pull out the greatest common factor
$$25x^2 - 49 = (5x + 7)(5x - 7)$$
Difference of squares: both terms are perfect squares, minus between
$$x^2 - 9x + 20 = (x - 4)(x - 5)$$
Trinomial: two numbers that multiply to $20$ and add to $-9$
Example

Which expression is equivalent to $16x^2 - 81$?

  1. A)$(4x + 9)(4x - 9)$
  2. B)$(4x - 9)^2$
  3. C)$(4x + 9)^2$
  4. D)$(8x - 9)(2x + 9)$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Difference of squares: $(4x)^2 - 9^2$. Number check, $x = 1$: original $16 - 81 = -65$; this gives $(13)(-5) = -65$. ✓
  • Choice B: $(4x - 9)^2 = 16x^2 - 72x + 81$: a middle term appears and the constant's sign flips.
  • Choice C: $(4x + 9)^2 = 16x^2 + 72x + 81$: same problems, other direction.
  • Choice D: The outer terms multiply correctly ($16x^2$, $-81$), but the cross terms don't cancel: this expands to $16x^2 + 54x - 81$.
A two-term expression with a minus between two perfect squares is always a difference of squares—$16x^2 - 81$, $x^4 - 25$, even $9 - y^2$. Spot the pattern, skip the work.
Advanced MathEquivalent Expressions: Exponent Rules

Exponent Rules

Exponent questions are pure rule application. Each rule is just repeated multiplication counted carefully:

RuleStatementExample
Product$x^a \cdot x^b = x^{a+b}$$x^3 \cdot x^5 = x^8$
Quotient$\dfrac{x^a}{x^b} = x^{a-b}$$\dfrac{x^7}{x^2} = x^5$
Power of a power$(x^a)^b = x^{ab}$$(x^4)^2 = x^8$
Power of a product$(xy)^a = x^a y^a$$(3x)^2 = 9x^2$
Negative exponent$x^{-a} = \dfrac{1}{x^a}$$x^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$
Rational exponent$x^{a/b} = \sqrt[b]{x^a}$$x^{1/2} = \sqrt{x}$
Zero exponent$x^0 = 1$ (for $x \neq 0$)$5^0 = 1$
Example

Which expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{(3x^4)^2 \cdot x}{x^5}$?

$$\frac{9x^8 \cdot x}{x^5}$$
Power of a product: square the $3$ and the $x^4$
$$\frac{9x^9}{x^5}$$
Product rule: $x^8 \cdot x^1 = x^9$
$$9x^4$$
Quotient rule: subtract exponents
Example

For $x > 0$, which expression is equivalent to $x^{5/2}$?

  1. A)$\sqrt{x^5}$
  2. B)$\sqrt[5]{x^2}$
  3. C)$x^5 \div x^2$
  4. D)$\tfrac{5}{2}x$
Process of Elimination:
  • Choice A: Denominator = root, numerator = power: $x^{5/2} = \sqrt{x^5}$. Check $x = 4$: $4^{5/2} = 32$ and $\sqrt{4^5} = \sqrt{1024} = 32$. ✓
  • Choice B: Flips the fraction's roles: this is $x^{2/5}$, a fifth root, not a square root.
  • Choice C: Reads the fraction as division of powers: $x^5 \div x^2 = x^3$, and $x^3 \neq x^{5/2}$.
  • Choice D: Multiplies by the exponent—exponents aren't coefficients.
A negative exponent doesn't make anything negative—it flips position: $x^{-2} = \frac{1}{x^2}$, which is positive for every $x \neq 0$.
Advanced MathEquivalent Expressions: Rational Expressions & Matching Coefficients

Rational Expressions & Matching Coefficients

Simplify Fractions by Factoring

Rule: A fraction of polynomials simplifies only by canceling common factors. Factor the top, factor the bottom, then cancel whole parentheses—never individual terms.

Example

For $x \neq -2$ and $x \neq -3$, which expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 5x + 6}$?

$$\frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x + 2)(x + 3)}$$
Factor: difference of squares on top, trinomial below
$$\frac{x - 3}{x + 2}$$
Cancel the common factor $(x + 3)$
  • $\dfrac{(x - 3)\cancel{(x + 3)}}{(x + 2)\cancel{(x + 3)}} = \dfrac{x - 3}{x + 2}$
    Whole factors cancel. Check $x = 1$: original $\frac{-8}{12} = -\frac{2}{3}$; simplified $\frac{-2}{3}$. ✓
  • “Cancel the $x^2$s”: $\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 5x + 6} \to \dfrac{-9}{5x + 6}$
    $x^2$ is a term, not a factor—crossing it out changes the value. At $x = 1$ this gives $-\frac{9}{11} \neq -\frac{2}{3}$.

Matching Coefficients

If two polynomials are equivalent for all $x$, their coefficients match power by power—that's how the SAT asks you to find unknown constants.

Example

The equation $3x^2 + kx - 10 = (3x - 5)(x + c)$ is true for all values of $x$, where $k$ and $c$ are constants. What is the value of $k$?

$$(3x - 5)(x + c) = 3x^2 + (3c - 5)x - 5c$$
Expand the right side
$$-5c = -10 \;\Rightarrow\; c = 2$$
Match the constant terms
$$k = 3c - 5 = 1$$
Match the $x$-coefficients

Check with $x = 1$: left side $3 + 1 - 10 = -6$; right side $(3 - 5)(1 + 2) = -6$. ✓

“True for all values of $x$” is the signal phrase for coefficient matching. Expand, then line up: $x^2$ with $x^2$, $x$ with $x$, constant with constant.
Advanced MathEquivalent Expressions: Practice

Practice

Work each one by rule—then, before opening the answer, verify your choice with a quick $x = 2$ test. Two independent paths to the same answer is how you know.

Practice
  1. 1.

    Which expression is equivalent to $(4x^2 - 2x + 5) - (x^2 + 3x - 7)$?

    1. A)$3x^2 - 5x + 12$
    2. B)$3x^2 - 5x - 2$
    3. C)$3x^2 + x - 2$
    4. D)$5x^2 + x - 2$

    The correct answer is A. The minus distributes to all three terms of the second polynomial:

    $$4x^2 - 2x + 5 - x^2 - 3x + 7$$
    Flip every sign in the parentheses
    $$3x^2 - 5x + 12$$
    Combine like terms
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Test $x = 2$: original $(16 - 4 + 5) - (4 + 6 - 7) = 17 - 3 = 14$; this gives $12 - 10 + 12 = 14$. ✓
    • Choice B: Flipped $x^2$ and $3x$ but not the $-7$: the constant became $5 - 7 = -2$ instead of $5 + 7 = 12$.
    • Choice C: Flipped nothing: it added $3x$ and $-7$ as written.
    • Choice D: Added the polynomials outright—the leading term $5x^2$ gives it away instantly.
  2. 2.

    Which expression is equivalent to $(2x + 5)^2$?

    1. A)$4x^2 + 20x + 25$
    2. B)$4x^2 + 25$
    3. C)$4x^2 + 10x + 25$
    4. D)$2x^2 + 20x + 25$

    The correct answer is A. Square of a sum: $a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ with $a = 2x$, $b = 5$.

    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: $(2x)^2 + 2(2x)(5) + 5^2$. Test $x = 1$: $(7)^2 = 49$ and $4 + 20 + 25 = 49$. ✓
    • Choice B: The classic: squares the pieces and skips the middle term $2ab$ entirely.
    • Choice C: Has a middle term, but forgot to double it: $2(2x)(5) = 20x$, not $10x$.
    • Choice D: Squared the $5$ but not the $2$: $(2x)^2 = 4x^2$, coefficient included.
  3. 3.

    Which expression is equivalent to $(2a^5)^3 \cdot a^{-2}$?

    1. A)$8a^{13}$
    2. B)$6a^{13}$
    3. C)$8a^{17}$
    4. D)$2a^{13}$

    The correct answer is A.

    $$(2a^5)^3 = 8a^{15}$$
    Cube the $2$ and multiply the exponents
    $$8a^{15} \cdot a^{-2} = 8a^{13}$$
    Product rule: add the exponents, $15 + (-2)$
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Both rules applied in order. ✓
    • Choice B: $6$ comes from $2 \times 3$—but the coefficient gets cubed, not multiplied by the exponent: $2^3 = 8$.
    • Choice C: $a^{17}$ adds $2$ instead of $-2$: a negative exponent subtracts in the product rule.
    • Choice D: Leaves the $2$ uncubed—the power outside applies to everything inside the parentheses.
  4. 4.

    For $x \neq -\tfrac{5}{2}$, which expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{6x^2 + 7x - 20}{2x + 5}$?

    1. A)$3x - 4$
    2. B)$3x + 4$
    3. C)$2x + 5$
    4. D)$6x - 4$

    The correct answer is A. Factor the numerator so the denominator can cancel:

    $$6x^2 + 7x - 20 = (2x + 5)(3x - 4)$$
    Check the middle: $2x(-4) + 5(3x) = 7x$ ✓
    $$\frac{(2x + 5)(3x - 4)}{2x + 5} = 3x - 4$$
    Cancel the common factor
    Process of Elimination:
    • Choice A: Test $x = 2$: original $\frac{24 + 14 - 20}{9} = \frac{18}{9} = 2$; and $3(2) - 4 = 2$. ✓
    • Choice B: Sign slip on the constant: $(2x + 5)(3x + 4)$ would end in $+20$, not $-20$.
    • Choice C: Returns the divisor—it cancels the wrong factor of the numerator.
    • Choice D: Term-by-term division ($6x^2$ by $x$, $-20$ by $5$)—division doesn't distribute over the denominator's terms.

Things to Remember

  • Equivalent = equal for every value. Verify any rewrite by plugging in an easy number (not $0$ or $1$).
  • Combine only like terms; a subtracted polynomial flips the sign of every term inside.
  • Special products: $(a \pm b)^2 = a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2$ — never drop the middle term — and $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2$.
  • Factor in this order: GCF first, then difference of squares, then trinomial patterns.
  • Exponents: product → add, quotient → subtract, power of a power → multiply; $x^{-a} = \frac{1}{x^a}$; $x^{a/b} = \sqrt[b]{x^a}$ (denominator is the root).
  • Fractions of polynomials: factor top and bottom, cancel whole factors—never lone terms.
  • “True for all $x$” → match coefficients power by power.
