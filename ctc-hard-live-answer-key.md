## 1. `CTC-H01`

Text 1

Some squids and octopuses alter certain RNA messages before proteins are made, so a single gene can yield more than one protein form. Biologist Ingrid Saetre notes that the altered positions have changed less across related species than nearby DNA has, especially in genes active in nervous tissue. She concludes that natural selection preserved those positions because the capacity to alter RNA benefits the animals.

Text 2

Biologist Tarek Boulos compares the same gene segments with those of nautiluses, which do not alter RNA at the positions Saetre studied. **Based on the texts, how would Boulos (Text 2) most likely respond to the inference Saetre draws in Text 1?**

- A) By accepting that RNA alteration is beneficial in squids and octopuses while arguing that nautiluses achieve the same benefit by a different process
- B) By disputing Saetre's observation that the altered positions have changed less across species than the DNA surrounding them
- C) By granting that the positions are conserved but arguing that the evidence cannot distinguish a benefit of RNA alteration from the importance of the unaltered protein
- D) By treating conservation in nautiluses as evidence that their ancestors altered RNA at the same positions and later lost the capacity

**Answer: C** — By granting that the positions are conserved but arguing that the evidence cannot distinguish a benefit of RNA alteration from the importance of the unaltered protein
