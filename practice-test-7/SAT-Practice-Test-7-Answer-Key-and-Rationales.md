### Question 1 — C

**Choice C is the best answer** because in this context "entertained" most nearly means considered. The narrator holds in mind, for "a short moment," the possibility of making an effort to improve her situation, and the passage then reports that she "forgot my good intention," indicating that the idea had been under consideration rather than carried out.

Choice A is incorrect. Although in some contexts "entertained" can mean hosted, as when a person entertains visitors for an evening, the narrator is alone in her room, and what she entertains is an idea rather than a guest.
Choice B is incorrect. Although "entertained" can mean amused, as when a performer entertains an audience, the word here takes "the idea" as its object; the narrator is describing a thought she turns over, not amusement she provides or receives.
Choice D is incorrect. "Entertained" isn't used to mean announced, or stated publicly, and the narrator says nothing aloud: the intention stays in her mind until weariness drives it out.

**Choice D is the best answer** because it most logically completes the text. In this context, "reversible" means able to be undone. Choice C is incorrect because cost never enters the rule the text describes. Whether an adhesive is "inexpensive," or low in price, has no bearing on whether a later conservator can dissolve it and lift it away.

### Question 12 — C

### Question 13 — B

**Choice B is the best answer.** The text establishes that speakers whose language forbids a cluster like *bz* insert a vowel when they say such a word, and the study shows that those same speakers cannot tell a recording of *ebzo* from a recording of *ebuzo*. ### Question 14 — D

## Review process

Every item was attacked by two independent reviewers before assembly — one testing answer defensibility and factual accuracy, one auditing style fidelity against the measured corpus profile. Four items were found to have two defensible answers and were rebuilt; one literary passage was found to contain invented dialogue and was replaced outright; two Command of Evidence items were rewritten into the correct stimulus frame; one quantitative item contained a rate-versus-total arithmetic error and was restated. Six redundancy pairs and a cluster of over-used devices (a colon gloss in 9 of 9 Words in Context items, four consecutive role-first openers, shared leading clauses in every Inference item) were corrected in a second pass.
