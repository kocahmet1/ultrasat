### M1-Q9
SKILL: Central Ideas and Details | SUBTYPE: main idea | DIFFICULTY: Easy | TOPIC: Literature — poem | WORDS: 85
PASSAGE:
The following text is from the 1920 poem "The Tropics in New York" by Claude McKay, a Jamaican American writer. A rill is a small stream.

    Bananas ripe and green, and ginger-root,
    Cocoa in pods and alligator pears,
    And tangerines and mangoes and grape fruit,
    Fit for the highest prize at parish fairs,

    Set in the window, bringing memories
    Of fruit-trees laden by low-singing rills,
    And dewy dawns, and mystical blue skies
    In benediction over nun-like hills.

    My eyes grew dim, and I could no more gaze;
    A wave of longing through my body swept,
    And, hungry for the old, familiar ways,
    I turned aside and bowed my head and wept.
PROMPT: Which choice best states the main idea of the text?
A) The speaker is relieved to have put his old, familiar ways behind him.
B) Fruit displayed in a window stirs memories that leave the speaker overcome with longing.
C) The speaker intends to return home as soon as he can afford to.
D) The fruit in the window is fine enough to win prizes at fairs.
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer because it states the main idea of the text. The speaker sees tropical fruit set in a window, the fruit brings back memories of fruit-trees, dewy dawns, and blue skies, and the speaker, "hungry for the old, familiar ways," is so overcome that he weeps.
Choice A is incorrect. The text states the opposite of this. The speaker describes himself as "hungry for the old, familiar ways" and weeping over them, not as relieved to be free of them.
Choice C is incorrect. The text never mentions any plan to return, or anything about what the speaker can or cannot afford.
Choice D is incorrect. The text does say the fruit is "fit for the highest prize at parish fairs," but that's a detail about the fruit's appearance, not the main idea. The poem is mainly about what the sight of the fruit does to the speaker.

### M1-Q10
SKILL: Central Ideas and Details | SUBTYPE: detail | DIFFICULTY: Medium | TOPIC: Social science — archaeology | WORDS: 90
PASSAGE:
In the Andes, weavers have long spun thread from cotton, which grows along the Pacific coast, and from the fleece of highland camelids such as alpacas. Archaeologist Elvira Quisbert Mamani examined fragments of cloth from a coastal cemetery dating to 800 CE. Although the fragments' plain outer borders were cotton, the dyed patterned bands at their centers were camelid fiber. Quisbert concluded that the weavers, who lived hundreds of kilometers from any alpaca herd, likely reserved the scarcer imported material for the portions of a garment meant to be seen.
PROMPT: According to the text, what did the arrangement of fibers in the fragments indicate about the weavers?
A) The weavers reserved locally grown cotton for the most elaborate and visible portions of a garment.
B) The weavers obtained camelid fiber by trading their finished cotton cloth with communities in the highlands.
C) Dyes strong enough to color cotton fiber were not available to weavers living on the coast.
D) The weavers used the harder-to-obtain fiber for the parts of a finished garment that would be visible.
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer. Cotton grew locally on the coast, while camelid fiber had to come from herds hundreds of kilometers away, so camelid fiber was the scarcer, imported material. The text says that fiber appeared in the dyed patterned bands at the fragments' centers, and Quisbert concluded that the weavers reserved it for the parts of a garment meant to be seen.
Choice A is incorrect. The text states the opposite of this. Cotton was in the plain outer borders; the patterned centers were camelid fiber.
Choice B is incorrect because the text never says how the weavers obtained camelid fiber, and it makes no mention of trade of any kind.
Choice C is incorrect. This choice goes beyond the information in the text. The text says the patterned camelid bands were dyed, but it says nothing about whether dyes for cotton were available.

### M1-Q15
SKILL: Inferences | DIFFICULTY: Hard | TOPIC: Science — glaciology / climate | WORDS: 85
BLANK-FRAME: concluded that ______ (with connective "therefore")
PASSAGE:
A layer of rock debris on a glacier can either hasten melting, by absorbing sunlight the ice would otherwise reflect, or delay it, by insulating the ice; layers thinner than about two centimeters tend to do the former, thicker layers the latter. Glaciologist Solveig Bakken and her team compared two neighboring glaciers receiving nearly identical sunlight and snowfall but differing in debris cover: on one it has yet to reach two centimeters, and on the other it now exceeds twenty. Bakken's team therefore concluded that ______
PROMPT: Which choice most logically completes the text?
A) the glacier with the thinner debris cover is currently losing ice more rapidly than the one whose debris exceeds twenty centimeters.
B) the glacier with the thinner debris cover is currently better shielded from melting than the one whose debris exceeds twenty centimeters.
C) the glacier with the thinner debris cover will accumulate enough additional debris within a few decades to begin melting more slowly.
D) the glacier with the thinner debris cover is more typical of mountain ranges around the world than the other is.
CORRECT: A
RATIONALE:
Choice A is the best answer. The text says that debris thinner than about two centimeters tends to hasten melting while thicker debris tends to delay it, and that the two glaciers receive nearly identical sunlight and snowfall. It follows that the glacier whose debris has yet to reach two centimeters is melting faster than the one whose debris exceeds twenty.
Choice B is incorrect. This reverses the logic of the text. It's the thicker layer, not the thinner one, that insulates the ice and delays melting.
Choice C is incorrect. There's no basis for this inference. The text says nothing about whether debris is still accumulating on either glacier or how quickly it might do so.
Choice D is incorrect. It doesn't logically complete the text. The text compares only these two glaciers and never addresses how common either condition is elsewhere.

### M2-Q8
SKILL: Central Ideas and Details | SUBTYPE: "Based on the text, …" | DIFFICULTY: Medium | TOPIC: Literature — poem | WORDS: 89
PASSAGE:
The following text is from the 1905 poem "Indian Weavers" by Sarojini Naidu, an Indian poet and political activist. A halcyon is a kingfisher, a brightly colored bird.

    Weavers, weaving at break of day,
    Why do you weave a garment so gay? . . .
    Blue as the wing of a halcyon wild,
    We weave the robes of a new-born child.

    Weavers, weaving at fall of night,
    Why do you weave a garment so bright? . . .
    Like the plumes of a peacock, purple and green,
    We weave the marriage-veils of a queen.

    Weavers, weaving solemn and still,
    What do you weave in the moonlight chill? . . .
    White as a feather and white as a cloud,
    We weave a dead man's funeral shroud.
PROMPT: Based on the text, what is true of the garments the weavers describe?
A) As the day proceeds, the garments the weavers produce become steadily brighter and more festive in character.
B) The weavers take greater pride in the marriage-veils they weave than in any of their other work.
C) The garments the weavers make at different times of day correspond to different stages in a human life.
D) The weavers regard the work they do at night as more important than their daytime work.
CORRECT: C
RATIONALE:
Choice C is the best answer. At break of day the weavers make robes for a new-born child, at fall of night they make the marriage-veils of a queen, and in the moonlight they make a dead man's funeral shroud. Each garment belongs to a later point in a life than the one before it.
Choice A is incorrect. The text states the opposite of this. The first garment is "gay" and the second "bright," but the last is a white funeral shroud woven "solemn and still" in the "moonlight chill."
Choice B is incorrect because the text says nothing about the weavers' feelings toward any of the garments they make. They simply say what each one is for.
Choice D is incorrect. The text makes no comparison between the importance of the weavers' nighttime work and that of their daytime work.

### M2-Q13
SKILL: Inferences | DIFFICULTY: Medium | TOPIC: Linguistics | WORDS: 85
BLANK-FRAME: "This finding suggests that ______"
PASSAGE:
Every language allows only certain sequences of consonants, and speakers borrowing a foreign word often break up a forbidden sequence by inserting a vowel. Linguist Devendra Karki and his colleagues wondered whether this habit also affects what speakers hear. They played recordings of the invented words *ebzo* and *ebuzo*, in random order, to fluent speakers of a language that permits no consonant cluster like *bz*. Asked which of the two words they had just heard, listeners answered no better than chance. This finding suggests that ______
PROMPT: Which choice most logically completes the text?
A) the listeners' native language shapes their pronunciation of unfamiliar words but not their perception.
B) the listeners' native language shapes their perception of unfamiliar words as well as their pronunciation.
C) the listeners' native language shapes their memory for unfamiliar words rather than their perception of them.
D) the listeners' native language contains fewer consonant clusters than most of the world's other languages.
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer. The text establishes that speakers insert a vowel when they pronounce a forbidden consonant sequence. Because the listeners could not tell *ebzo* from *ebuzo* any better than they could by guessing, the pattern of their native language must be shaping what they hear and not only what they say.
Choice A is incorrect. This reverses the logic of the text. If the listeners' native language affected only pronunciation, they would have been able to hear the difference between the two recordings.
Choice C is incorrect. There's no basis for this inference. The text describes listeners identifying a word they had just heard, and it makes no claims about how well they remembered anything.
Choice D is incorrect. The text says the listeners' language permits no cluster like *bz*, but it never compares that language to any other language.

### M2-Q14
SKILL: Inferences | DIFFICULTY: Hard | TOPIC: Science — physics / acoustics | WORDS: 90
BLANK-FRAME: "thus reasoned that ______"
PASSAGE:
The ornate plaster moldings covering the walls of many nineteenth-century concert halls are often credited with the halls' warm sound: their irregular surfaces are thought to scatter reflected sound in many directions rather than returning it as one sharp echo. Others credit the halls' narrow, boxlike proportions instead. Physicist Amaya Sotomayor and her colleagues measured scattering in a hall whose moldings had been removed during a recent renovation but whose overall proportions were unchanged. Scattering there was nearly as strong as it had been before. Sotomayor's team thus reasoned that ______
PROMPT: Which choice most logically completes the text?
A) the hall's plaster moldings are responsible for nearly all of the scattering that occurs there, and its proportions for almost none of it.
B) the hall's plaster moldings would scatter sound far more effectively if they were rebuilt from the same materials the original builders had used.
C) the hall's plaster moldings were added for the sake of appearance by builders who had no way of predicting their effect on sound.
D) the hall's plaster moldings contribute far less to the scattering of sound there than do features that the renovation left in place.
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer. Removing the moldings left scattering nearly as strong as it had been before, so most of the scattering must come from something the renovation did not remove — the hall's proportions, for instance, which the text notes were unchanged.
Choice A is incorrect. This reverses the logic of the text. If the moldings produced nearly all the scattering, taking them away would have caused scattering to drop sharply rather than remain almost unchanged.
Choice B is incorrect. There's no basis for this inference. The text never says what the moldings were made of or how the material might affect the way they scatter sound.
Choice C is incorrect. The text doesn't address why the moldings were installed or what their builders knew, so there's no support for this claim.
