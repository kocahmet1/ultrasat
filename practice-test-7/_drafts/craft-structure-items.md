### M1-Q6
SKILL: Text Structure and Purpose
SUBTYPE: main purpose
DIFFICULTY: Medium
TOPIC: Literature — prose fiction
STIMULUS-WORDS: 95
CHOICE-OPENER: To
MEAN-CHOICE-WORDS: 14.5
TRAP-TYPES: A=fabricated content, B=X-not-Y substitution, D=true content wrong role
PASSAGE:
The following text is adapted from Thomas Hardy's 1872 novel *Under the Greenwood Tree*. In this text, a plantation is a grove of planted trees.

To dwellers in a wood almost every species of tree has its voice as well as its feature. At the passing of the breeze the fir-trees sob and moan no less distinctly than they rock; the holly whistles as it battles with itself; the ash hisses amid its quiverings; the beech rustles while its flat boughs rise and fall. And winter, which modifies the note of such trees as shed their leaves, does not destroy its individuality. On a starry Christmas Eve a man passed up a lane in the darkness of such a plantation.
PROMPT: Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?
A) To argue that winter changes a wood more profoundly than any other season does.
B) To describe the changing appearance of a wood as its trees shed their leaves.
C) To describe the distinct sounds by which the trees of a wood can be identified.
D) To introduce a character by depicting the wood he travels through on a winter night.
CORRECT: C
RATIONALE:
Choice C is the best answer. The main purpose of the text is to describe the distinct sounds by which the trees of a wood can be identified. The text opens by claiming that for "dwellers in a wood almost every species of tree has its voice as well as its feature," then supports that claim with a catalog of sounds: the fir-trees "sob and moan," the holly "whistles," the ash "hisses," and the beech "rustles." The text adds that winter alters but does not erase the individuality of these voices.
Choice A is incorrect because the text never compares winter with any other season. It states only that winter "modifies the note" of trees that lose their leaves without destroying each tree's individuality.
Choice B is incorrect because although the text mentions trees that shed their leaves, it describes how the trees of a wood sound rather than how they look.
Choice D is incorrect because although a man does appear in the text's final sentence, the descriptions of the trees' voices aren't presented as a means of characterizing him; the man is mentioned only after the discussion of the wood's sounds is complete, and the text supplies no details about him.

### M1-Q7
SKILL: Text Structure and Purpose
SUBTYPE: overall structure
DIFFICULTY: Hard
TOPIC: Science — ornithology / bird migration
STIMULUS-WORDS: 85
CHOICE-OPENER: It
MEAN-CHOICE-WORDS: 31
PASSAGE:
Songbirds making their first migration have no experienced companion to follow, and for decades researchers assumed that such birds rely on an inherited program specifying only a direction and a duration—a simple rule that would carry them, on average, to the right region. Tracking hand-raised buntings released far from their usual route, ornithologist Halima Diallo found that some individuals shifted course toward the correct wintering area, a correction the inherited-program account does not predict. Whether those birds were correcting or merely drifting, however, remains unclear.
PROMPT: Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?
A) It describes a method for raising migratory birds in captivity, then explains why that method produces unusually strong navigational performance, and then recommends that future studies adopt it more widely.
B) It summarizes a researcher's proposal about navigation in young birds, then describes the assumption that the proposal was meant to replace, and then identifies a difficulty that complicates its acceptance.
C) It outlines a widely held account of first-time migration, then presents findings that appear to conflict with that account, and then explains which of two competing interpretations the findings support.
D) It presents a long-standing assumption about how inexperienced migrants find their way, then reports an observation that the assumption does not account for, and then notes that the observation may admit another explanation.
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer because the text moves through three stages. It opens with the assumption researchers long made about how first-time migrants find their way—an inherited program specifying a direction and a duration—then reports Diallo's observation that some hand-raised buntings shifted course toward the correct wintering area, a correction that account does not predict, and then notes that it isn't clear whether those birds were correcting or drifting.
Choice A is incorrect because the text doesn't explain why hand-raising would produce strong navigational performance, and it makes no recommendation about future studies. The birds are described as hand-raised only to establish that they had no migratory experience.
Choice B is incorrect because it reverses the order of the text and misassigns its ideas. The text begins with the long-standing assumption rather than with Diallo's work, and Diallo is credited with an observation, not with a proposal advanced to replace that assumption.
Choice C is incorrect because although the text does present a widely held account and then findings that conflict with it, it doesn't explain which interpretation those findings support. Instead, it states that the choice between the two readings remains unclear.

### M1-Q8
SKILL: Cross-Text Connections
SUBTYPE: response to the underlined claim
DIFFICULTY: Hard
TOPIC: Science — soil carbon / regenerative agriculture
STIMULUS-WORDS: 131 (Text 1 = 62, Text 2 = 69)
CHOICE-OPENER: By
MEAN-CHOICE-WORDS: 25
PASSAGE:
Text 1
Converting cropland to no-till management—leaving crop residue on the surface rather than plowing it under—is among the most frequently recommended agricultural strategies for slowing the rise of atmospheric carbon dioxide. _Some agronomists hold that fields managed without tillage steadily accumulate additional carbon in their soils._ Farmers across several major grain-growing regions have adopted the practice partly on the strength of that expectation.

Text 2
Soil scientist Ana Sofía Berrocal and colleagues sampled paired tilled and untilled fields to a depth of one meter, well below the top thirty centimeters that most surveys examine. Carbon in the untilled fields was indeed concentrated near the surface, as advocates of the practice predict. Below that layer, though, carbon stocks were lower than in the tilled fields, so that full-profile totals differed little between the two systems.

UNDERLINED CLAIM (Text 1): "Some agronomists hold that fields managed without tillage steadily accumulate additional carbon in their soils."
PROMPT: Based on the texts, how would Berrocal and colleagues (Text 2) most likely respond to the underlined claim in Text 1?
A) By acknowledging that untilled soils gain carbon at shallow depths but noting that this gain is offset in deeper layers most surveys never reach.
B) By acknowledging that untilled soils gain carbon at shallow depths but insisting that plowing is therefore the more effective way to store carbon in cropland.
C) By acknowledging that untilled soils gain carbon at shallow depths but arguing that the carbon missing from deeper layers has been released into the atmosphere.
D) By acknowledging that untilled soils gain carbon at shallow depths but cautioning that few growers have maintained the practice long enough for totals to matter.
CORRECT: A
RATIONALE:
Choice A is the best answer because Berrocal and colleagues would grant the surface accumulation while denying that it amounts to a net gain. Their sampling confirmed that carbon in untilled fields was concentrated near the surface, but it also found lower stocks below the top thirty centimeters that most surveys examine, so that full-profile totals differed little between tilled and untilled fields.
Choice B is incorrect because this choice overstates the position of Text 2. Berrocal and colleagues report that totals for the two systems differed little, which gives no basis for claiming that tillage stores carbon more effectively than no-till management does.
Choice C is incorrect because Text 2 doesn't say what became of the carbon absent from the deeper layers of untilled fields. It reports a difference in where carbon sits within the soil profile, not a release of carbon into the atmosphere.
Choice D is incorrect because neither text mentions how long farmers have maintained no-till management or indicates that the duration of the practice bears on the measurements Berrocal and colleagues report.

### M2-Q6
SKILL: Text Structure and Purpose
SUBTYPE: function of underlined sentence
DIFFICULTY: Medium
TOPIC: Literature — prose fiction
STIMULUS-WORDS: 95
CHOICE-OPENER: It
MEAN-CHOICE-WORDS: 15
PASSAGE:
The following text is adapted from Charles W. Chesnutt's 1898 short story "The Wife of His Youth." Mr. Ryder belongs to the Blue Vein Society, a social club in the city of Groveland.

Mr. Ryder had come to Groveland as a young man and had begun as a messenger in the office of a railroad company. _By steady application he had risen to a clerkship, saved his earnings, bought a neat house on a shaded street, and filled its shelves with the works of the poets._ The members of the Blue Vein Society, who prized cultivation above every other advantage, thought him the most correct man among them and made him their dean. The ball he was now planning would be the finest the society had ever given.
UNDERLINED SENTENCE: "By steady application he had risen to a clerkship, saved his earnings, bought a neat house on a shaded street, and filled its shelves with the works of the poets."
PROMPT: Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?
A) It explains why a character chose to leave his first employer for a better position.
B) It emphasizes the importance that a character's club placed on refinement and personal cultivation.
C) It describes a character's rise from the position just mentioned to the standing his club admires.
D) It presents the reading habits that prepared a character for the event he is planning.
CORRECT: C
RATIONALE:
Choice C is the best answer because the underlined sentence follows Mr. Ryder from the messenger's job named in the previous sentence to a clerkship, savings, a house, and a shelf of poetry—precisely the kind of cultivation that the next sentence identifies as what the Blue Vein Society prizes above every other advantage and as the basis for its regard for him.
Choice A is incorrect because the text doesn't indicate that Mr. Ryder left the railroad company or give any reason for a change of employer. The underlined sentence describes his advancement within the office he entered as a messenger, not a departure from it.
Choice B is incorrect because although the text does state that the society prized cultivation above every other advantage, that point is made in the sentence following the underlined one. The underlined sentence reports what Mr. Ryder himself accomplished and never mentions the society or its values.
Choice D is incorrect because the underlined sentence mentions the poets only as one item in a list of what Mr. Ryder acquired. Nothing in the text connects his reading to the ball he is planning.

### M2-Q7
SKILL: Text Structure and Purpose
SUBTYPE: main purpose
DIFFICULTY: Hard
TOPIC: Social science — urban ecology / city planning
STIMULUS-WORDS: 86
CHOICE-OPENER: To
MEAN-CHOICE-WORDS: 18
PASSAGE:
Planners often justify new urban parks by their cooling effect—shade and evaporation lower nearby air temperatures—and assume that a city's total green area determines how much summer heat residents endure. Comparing temperature readings from neighborhoods with equal park acreage but different layouts, urban ecologist Thandiwe Mabaso found that many small, dispersed green spaces cooled a district's nights more than a single large park of the same acreage did. The arrangement of greenery within a district, her results suggest, may matter as much as its total extent.
PROMPT: Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?
A) To explain the physical processes by which parks reduce air temperatures in the neighborhoods around them.
B) To present findings suggesting that a widely held assumption about urban cooling overlooks how green space is arranged.
C) To argue that city planners should replace their largest existing parks with networks of much smaller green spaces.
D) To compare the total acreage of green space available in districts that differ in their summer nighttime temperatures.
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer because the text first states the assumption underlying a common justification for urban parks—that a city's total green area determines how much heat residents endure—and then reports Mabaso's comparison of districts holding acreage equal while varying layout, in which dispersed green spaces cooled a district's nights more than one large park did. The text closes by drawing out the implication that arrangement may matter as much as extent.
Choice A is incorrect because although the text does mention shade and evaporation, it does so in a single clause identifying why planners value parks; it doesn't develop an account of how that cooling works. The text's attention is on what Mabaso's comparison indicates about the placement of green space.
Choice C is incorrect because the text makes no recommendation about what cities should do. It reports a comparison and the tentative implication Mabaso draws from it, and it never suggests that existing parks be replaced.
Choice D is incorrect because acreage was held equal in the comparison the text describes, not varied. The districts Mabaso compared had the same park acreage and differed in how that acreage was laid out.
