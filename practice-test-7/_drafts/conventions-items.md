### M1-Q16
SKILL: SEC — Boundaries | RULE: supplementary element, matching-pair | DIFFICULTY: Easy | TOPIC: Arts — jazz | WORDS: 42
MENU: {dash, comma, semicolon, none}
FORCING-DEVICE: an em dash is already printed before the supplement, so only a second dash matches
PASSAGE:
The American musician Melba Liston is often remembered as a trombonist, but she was equally accomplished as an arranger. Her arrangements for pianist Randy Weston's 1960 album *Uhuru Afrika*—a suite honoring African independence ______ showcase her gift for lush brass writing.
PROMPT: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) movements,
B) movements
C) movements—
D) movements;
CORRECT: C
RATIONALE:
Choice C is the best answer. The convention being tested is the use of punctuation to set off a supplementary element. The supplementary element "a suite honoring African independence movements" offers additional information about the album *Uhuru Afrika*, and it must be separated from the rest of the sentence by a matching pair of punctuation marks. Because a dash appears before "a suite," a dash is also needed after "movements."
Choice A is incorrect because a comma can't be paired with a dash in this way to separate the supplementary element from the rest of the sentence.
Choice B is incorrect because it results in unmatched punctuation; the dash before "a suite" has no corresponding mark after "movements."
Choice D is incorrect because a semicolon can't be paired with a dash in this way to separate the supplementary element from the rest of the sentence.

### M1-Q17

In the 1990s, the entomologist Masato Ono documented an unusual defense that Japanese honeybees use against giant hornets. When a hornet scout approaches a hive, hundreds of bees ______ the intruder and vibrate their flight muscles, generating heat that is lethal to the larger insect.

Choice C is incorrect because the singular verb "has surrounded" doesn't agree in number with the plural subject "hundreds of bees."
Choice D is incorrect because the singular verb "is surrounding" doesn't agree in number with the plural subject "hundreds of bees."

### M1-Q18
SKILL: SEC — Boundaries | RULE: no punctuation (noun + restrictive relative clause) | DIFFICULTY: Easy | TOPIC: Arts — architecture | WORDS: 48
PASSAGE:
Opened in 1968, the São Paulo Museum of Art was designed by Italian-born Brazilian architect Lina Bo Bardi, who wanted to leave the ground beneath the building open. The four red concrete ______ lift the galleries above a plaza have since become a symbol of the city.
PROMPT: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) pillars; that
B) pillars. That
C) pillars, that
D) pillars that
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer. The convention being tested is the use of no punctuation between a noun and the restrictive clause that modifies it. The clause "that lift the galleries above a plaza" identifies which pillars are being discussed, so no punctuation is needed between it and the noun phrase "the four red concrete pillars."
Choice A is incorrect because a semicolon can only separate two main clauses, and "the four red concrete pillars" is a sentence fragment rather than a main clause.
Choice B is incorrect because it results in a sentence fragment; "the four red concrete pillars" has no finite verb and can't stand on its own as a sentence.
Choice C is incorrect because it results in an intrusive comma between a noun and the restrictive clause that identifies it.

### M1-Q19
SKILL: SEC — Form, Structure, and Sense | RULE: verb tense (past perfect) | DIFFICULTY: Medium | TOPIC: Science — chemistry / catalysis | WORDS: 50
PASSAGE:
Metal phthalocyanines, ring-shaped molecules that absorb light, drew the attention of South African chemist Tebello Nyokong in the 1980s. By the time she began adapting the compounds for cancer therapy in the 2000s, she ______ them as catalysts—substances that speed up chemical reactions—for breaking down industrial pollutants.
PROMPT: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) tests
B) had tested
C) will have been testing
D) will test
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer. The convention being tested is the use of verb tense to indicate the sequence of past events. The past perfect verb "had tested" shows that Nyokong's work on the compounds as catalysts was already complete by the time she "began" adapting them for cancer therapy.
Choice A is incorrect because the present tense verb "tests" isn't consistent with the past tense verbs "drew" and "began" used to describe events that occurred in the past.
Choice C is incorrect because the future perfect progressive verb "will have been testing" illogically presents research completed in the past as action continuing into the future.
Choice D is incorrect because the future tense verb "will test" isn't consistent with the past tense verbs used to describe Nyokong's earlier work.

### M1-Q20

A) Kanehsatà:ke, and
B) Kanehsatà:ke and
C) Kanehsatà:ke,
D) Kanehsatà:ke
CORRECT: A
RATIONALE:

### M1-Q21

PASSAGE:
The RoboBee, a flying robot built at Harvard's Microrobotics Laboratory, weighs about a tenth of a gram (less than a paper clip) and cannot carry a battery on board, so its designers supply a steady current through a wire thinner than a human hair, ______ the machine to a wall socket on the laboratory floor.
PROMPT: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) it connects
B) connects
C) it will connect
D) connecting
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer. The convention being tested is the use of a non-finite verb form within a supplementary element. The main clause "its designers supply a steady current through a wire thinner than a human hair" already contains the finite verb "supply," so the participle "connecting" is needed to introduce a supplementary phrase describing the result.
Choice A is incorrect because it results in a comma splice; "it connects the machine to a wall socket on the laboratory floor" is a main clause, and two main clauses can't be joined in this way with only a comma.
Choice B is incorrect because the finite verb "connects" has no subject and can't be joined to the finite verb "supply" with only a comma.
Choice C is incorrect because it results in a comma splice; "it will connect the machine to a wall socket on the laboratory floor" is a main clause, and two main clauses can't be joined in this way with only a comma.

### M1-Q22
SKILL: SEC — Boundaries | RULE: colon introducing a list | DIFFICULTY: Hard | TOPIC: Science — radio astronomy | WORDS: 58
PASSAGE:
The Murchison Radio-astronomy Observatory, known by its Wajarri name Inyarrimanha Ilgari Bundara, or "sharing sky and stars," sits in a legally protected radio-quiet zone in Western Australia that shields its antennas from three everyday sources of ______ mobile phones, car ignitions, and microwave ovens, all of which can swamp the faint signals from galaxies billions of light-years away.
PROMPT: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A) interference;
B) interference:
C) interference,
D) interference
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer. The convention being tested is the use of a colon to introduce a list. The words before the blank form a main clause ending in "three everyday sources of interference," and the colon signals that the items that follow specify what those three sources are.
Choice A is incorrect because a semicolon can't be used in this way to introduce a list; "mobile phones, car ignitions, and microwave ovens" is a sentence fragment rather than a main clause.
Choice C is incorrect because a comma illogically suggests that "three everyday sources of interference" is the first item in a list that also includes mobile phones, car ignitions, and microwave ovens.
Choice D is incorrect because punctuation is required after "interference" to separate the main clause from the list of sources it introduces.

### M2-Q21
SKILL: SEC — Form, Structure, and Sense | RULE: subject-modifier placement | DIFFICULTY: Hard | TOPIC: Science — epidemiology | WORDS: 58
PASSAGE:
Yellow fever swept through the port city of Havana repeatedly during the nineteenth century, killing thousands and baffling physicians, who could not explain how the disease traveled. In 1881, after observing that outbreaks rose and fell with the seasons rather than with the arrival of ships and cargo, and that those who nursed the sick rarely fell ill, ______
PROMPT: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

B) the Cuban physician Carlos Finlay's hypothesis was that a mosquito, not contaminated cargo, carried the disease.
C) the disease's true carrier, Carlos Finlay argued, was a mosquito rather than contaminated cargo.
D) the Cuban physician Carlos Finlay argued that a mosquito, not contaminated cargo, carried the disease.
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer. The convention being tested is the placement of a modifier. This choice makes "the Cuban physician Carlos Finlay" the subject of the main clause, which correctly indicates that it was Finlay who was "observing that outbreaks rose and fell with the seasons."
Choice A is incorrect because it results in a dangling modifier; the subject of the main clause, "a mosquito," wasn't observing anything.
Choice B is incorrect because it results in a dangling modifier; the subject of the main clause, "hypothesis," wasn't observing anything.
Choice C is incorrect because it results in a dangling modifier; the subject of the main clause, "the disease's true carrier," wasn't observing anything.
