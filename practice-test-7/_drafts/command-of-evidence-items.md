### M1-Q11
SKILL: Command of Evidence
SUBTYPE: literary quotation
DIFFICULTY: Easy
TOPIC: Literature — public-domain short story
PROSE-WORDS: 48
PASSAGE:
In Stephen Crane's 1897 short story "The Open Boat," four men adrift in a small dinghy struggle to reach shore after a shipwreck. A student claims that Crane emphasizes the men's isolation by describing waves so large that they block the men's view of everything beyond the boat.
PROMPT: Which quotation from "The Open Boat" most effectively illustrates the claim?
A) "The birds sat comfortably in groups, and they were envied by some in the dinghy, for the wrath of the sea was no more to them than it was to a covey of prairie chickens a thousand miles inland."
B) "As each slaty wall of water approached, it shut all else from the view of the men in the boat, and it was not difficult to imagine that this particular wave was the final outburst of the ocean, the last effort of the grim water."
C) "A seat in this boat was not unlike a seat upon a bucking broncho, and, by the same token, a broncho is not much smaller. The craft pranced and reared, and plunged like an animal."
D) "The cook squatted in the bottom and looked with both eyes at the six inches of gunwale which separated him from the ocean. His sleeves were rolled over his fat forearms, and the two flaps of his unbuttoned vest dangled as he bent to bail out the boat."
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer because the quotation states that each approaching wall of water "shut all else from the view of the men in the boat," which is exactly the claim that the waves are large enough to block the men's view of everything beyond the dinghy.
Choice A is incorrect because the quotation describes the men's envy of seabirds resting untroubled on the water; it makes no mention of the waves obstructing what the men could see.
Choice C is incorrect. Although the quotation vividly conveys the violence of the boat's motion, it says nothing about the men's view being blocked by the waves.
Choice D is incorrect because the quotation describes the cook's position in the boat and his appearance while bailing; it doesn't present the waves as cutting off the men's view of anything.

### M1-Q12
SKILL: Command of Evidence
SUBTYPE: quantitative — table
DIFFICULTY: Medium
TOPIC: Science — freshwater biology
PROSE-WORDS: 73
FIGURE:
Caption: Mean Shell Length and Mean Filtration Rate of Five Freshwater Mussel Species in One River Reach

| Species | Mean shell length (cm) | Mean filtration rate (liters per day) |
|---|---|---|
| Fatmucket | 9.4 | 22.6 |
| Threeridge | 11.2 | 18.3 |
| Plain pocketbook | 10.1 | 27.5 |
| Giant floater | 13.8 | 15.1 |
| Wabash pigtoe | 7.6 | 24.9 |

PASSAGE:
Freshwater mussels improve water quality by drawing river water across their gills and removing algae and suspended sediment. Because larger animals generally process more water, biologists have often estimated a mussel bed's filtering capacity from the size of the shells it contains. Limnologist Mariela Ocampo Duarte measured filtration rates for five species along a single stretch of river and concluded that shell length can be a poor guide to filtering capacity: for instance, ______
PROMPT: Which choice most effectively uses data from the table to complete the example?
A) the giant floater, the largest of the five species by shell length, had the lowest mean filtration rate.
B) the fatmucket had a greater mean shell length than the threeridge but a lower mean filtration rate.
C) the plain pocketbook had both a greater mean shell length and a greater mean filtration rate than the Wabash pigtoe had.
D) the Wabash pigtoe, at 11.2 centimeters, had a greater mean shell length than any other species in the study.
CORRECT: A
RATIONALE:
Choice A is the best answer because the giant floater has the greatest mean shell length in the table, 13.8 centimeters, but the lowest mean filtration rate, 15.1 liters per day. That pairing is precisely the kind of mismatch Ocampo Duarte's conclusion describes.
Choice B is incorrect. This choice misreads the table. The fatmucket's mean shell length, 9.4 centimeters, is less than the threeridge's 11.2 centimeters, and its mean filtration rate, 22.6 liters per day, is greater than the threeridge's 18.3 liters per day.
Choice C is incorrect. This accurately describes data from the table, but it doesn't support the conclusion. It presents a case in which the larger species also filters more water, which is what estimating capacity from shell size would predict.
Choice D is incorrect because, according to the table, the Wabash pigtoe's mean shell length is 7.6 centimeters, the smallest of the five species; 11.2 centimeters is the threeridge's mean shell length, and the greatest value in that column is the giant floater's 13.8 centimeters.

### M1-Q13
SKILL: Command of Evidence
SUBTYPE: hypothetical finding — support
DIFFICULTY: Hard
TOPIC: Science — sleep / physiology
PROSE-WORDS: 84
PASSAGE:
At the onset of sleep, the human body's core temperature falls by roughly a degree Celsius, largely because blood vessels in the hands and feet widen and release heat through the skin. People with insomnia often need an hour or more to fall asleep, and researchers have generally attributed that delay to heightened mental arousal at bedtime. Physiologist Amara Nwachukwu and colleagues hypothesized that for some of these individuals the delay instead reflects a failure of the hands and feet to shed enough heat.
PROMPT: Which finding, if true, would most directly support Nwachukwu and colleagues' hypothesis?
A) Participants with insomnia reported more worry during the hour before bed than other participants did, and their heart rates during that hour were higher.
B) Participants with insomnia and participants without insomnia reached similar core body temperatures within two hours after they had fallen asleep.
C) Participants with insomnia who exercised in the early evening showed a larger drop in core body temperature at bedtime than those who did not exercise.
D) Participants with insomnia fell asleep sooner on nights when they wore warmed socks and mittens, a treatment that increases blood flow to the hands and feet.
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer because warming the socks and mittens increases the blood flow that carries heat to the skin of the hands and feet. If shortening that pathway's limitation also shortens the time these participants need to fall asleep, the delay may indeed stem from insufficient heat release, as Nwachukwu and colleagues hypothesized.
Choice A is incorrect because a finding that participants with insomnia were more worried and had higher heart rates before bed would support the mental arousal explanation that Nwachukwu and colleagues set aside, not the explanation they proposed.
Choice B is incorrect. Core temperature measured after participants had already fallen asleep doesn't tell us anything about why some of them took longer to fall asleep in the first place, which is what the hypothesis is concerned with.
Choice C is incorrect because this finding concerns the effect of evening exercise on core temperature. It doesn't indicate whether heat released through the hands and feet affects how long participants with insomnia need to fall asleep.

A transit agency's biggest expenses are vehicles and driver hours, and it must add both when buses move slowly. Transportation economist Ravi Deshpande studied five corridors in one city where a lane of general traffic was converted to a dedicated bus lane in 2019, open to buses only during weekday rush hours. He concluded that the corridors that had been most expensive to operate before the change were the ones that showed the largest savings afterward: ______
PROMPT: Which choice most effectively uses data from the graph to complete the text?
A) Palmetto Way's cost per passenger trip decreased from $4.40 in 2018 to $4.20 in 2021.
B) Delaney Street had the lowest cost per passenger trip in 2021, at $3.50.
C) Sixth Avenue, which at $6.40 per trip had the highest cost in 2018, showed the largest decrease, to $4.30 in 2021.

Choice C is the best answer because Sixth Avenue had the highest 2018 cost per passenger trip of the five corridors, $6.40, and its drop to $4.30 in 2021 is a decrease of $2.10, the largest decrease shown. That is exactly the pattern Deshpande's conclusion describes.
Choice A is incorrect. This choice misreads the graph. Palmetto Way's cost per passenger trip rose from $4.20 in 2018 to $4.40 in 2021.

Choice D is incorrect because, according to the graph, Kestrel Road's cost per passenger trip fell from $4.80 to $4.20, a decrease of $0.60 rather than more than a dollar.

### M2-Q9
SKILL: Command of Evidence
SUBTYPE: quotation — nonfiction source
DIFFICULTY: Easy
TOPIC: Arts & humanities — 19th-century naturalist's published journals
PROSE-WORDS: 54
PASSAGE:
In *The Malay Archipelago* (1869), the naturalist Alfred Russel Wallace compiled the journals he kept during eight years of collecting insects and birds in Southeast Asia. A student claims that Wallace presents the physical effects of a single discovery as being as powerful as those he had felt when his life was in danger.
PROMPT: Which quotation from *The Malay Archipelago* most effectively illustrates the claim?
A) "The beauty and brilliancy of this insect are indescribable, and none but a naturalist can understand the intense excitement I experienced when I at length captured it."
B) "On taking it out of my net and opening the glorious wings, my heart began to beat violently, the blood rushed to my head, and I felt much more like fainting than I have done when in apprehension of immediate death."
C) "A rich butter-like custard highly flavoured with almonds gives the best general idea of it, but intermingled with it come wafts of flavour that call to mind cream-cheese, onion-sauce, brown sherry, and other incongruities."
D) "It seems sad that on the one hand such exquisite creatures should live out their lives and exhibit their charms only in these wild inhospitable regions, doomed for ages yet to come to hopeless barbarism."
CORRECT: B
RATIONALE:
Choice B is the best answer because the quotation catalogs bodily symptoms — a violently beating heart, blood rushing to the head, near-fainting — and then measures them against what Wallace had felt "in apprehension of immediate death," which is the comparison the student describes.
Choice A is incorrect. Although the quotation reports that Wallace felt intense excitement upon capturing the insect, it describes no physical effects on him and makes no comparison to an occasion when his life was in danger.
Choice C is incorrect because the quotation describes the flavor of a fruit rather than Wallace's response to a discovery, and it makes no reference to danger.
Choice D is incorrect because the quotation expresses regret about the remote places in which such creatures live rather than describing any effect the sight of them had on Wallace himself.

Most surveys of flower visitation are carried out in daylight, when bees and butterflies are active and easy to observe. Ecologist Wen-Chi Liao and colleagues used motion-triggered cameras to record visits to four wildflower species around the clock at a Wisconsin prairie. For one species, fewer than a fifth of all recorded visits occurred during daylight, a result that suggests daytime surveys may badly undercount its pollinators: ______
PROMPT: Which choice most effectively uses data from the graph to complete the text?
A) Evening primrose received a mean of 3 visits per hour during daylight and 21 visits per hour after dark.
B) White campion received a mean of 11 visits per hour during daylight and 9 visits per hour after dark.

### M2-Q11
SKILL: Command of Evidence
SUBTYPE: hypothetical finding — weaken
DIFFICULTY: Hard
TOPIC: Social science — archaeology (Pacific voyaging)
PROSE-WORDS: 91
PASSAGE:
Basalt is not found on low coral atolls, yet stone adzes made of basalt turn up in archaeological deposits on atolls across the central Pacific. The chemistry of the rock can be matched to particular quarries, and dozens of adzes from one atoll have been traced to a quarry on a high island some 2,500 kilometers away. Radiocarbon dates place that atoll's first settlement at about 1000 CE. Archaeologist Malia Fonoti and colleagues concluded that the atoll's residents maintained regular two-way voyaging with the quarry island for centuries after settling there.
PROMPT: Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken Fonoti and colleagues' conclusion?
A) The basalt quarry on the distant high island shows evidence of having been worked more or less continuously for over a thousand years.
B) A small number of the adzes recovered on the atoll were shaped from giant clam shell rather than from basalt of any kind.
C) Some adzes recovered on the atoll have shapes matching styles that were in use on the quarry island several centuries after the atoll was settled.
D) Every basalt adze recovered on the atoll came from deposits that formed within a few decades of the atoll's initial settlement.
CORRECT: D
RATIONALE:
Choice D is the best answer because Fonoti and colleagues concluded that voyaging continued for centuries after settlement. If every basalt adze on the atoll comes from deposits formed within a few decades of settlement, the stone could have arrived with the settlers themselves on a single founding voyage, and no later trips would be needed to account for it.
Choice A is incorrect because a long record of quarrying on the distant island indicates only that people there kept working the stone. It doesn't indicate whether anyone from the atoll continued to travel to that island.
Choice B is incorrect because the presence of a few shell adzes concerns what materials the atoll's residents used when basalt was unavailable; it doesn't bear on whether contact with the quarry island continued after settlement.
Choice C is incorrect because a finding that some adzes match styles used on the quarry island centuries after settlement would strengthen, not weaken, Fonoti and colleagues' conclusion, since it would suggest that stone was still arriving long after the atoll was first occupied.

### M2-Q12
SKILL: Command of Evidence
SUBTYPE: quantitative — table
DIFFICULTY: Hard
TOPIC: Science — permafrost / climate
PROSE-WORDS: 77
FIGURE:
Caption: Summer Air Temperature, Moss Thickness, and Active-Layer Depth at Five Arctic Tundra Sites, 2023

| Site | Mean summer air temperature (°C) | Moss layer thickness (cm) | Active-layer depth (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site A | 7.2 | 2 | 78 |
| Site B | 8.9 | 14 | 52 |
| Site C | 6.4 | 3 | 71 |
| Site D | 9.5 | 18 | 46 |
| Site E | 7.8 | 8 | 63 |

PASSAGE:
Permafrost—ground that stays frozen year-round—is capped by an active layer that thaws each summer; the deeper that layer, the more old carbon soil microbes can break down and release. Warmer summers push the active layer deeper, but the moss mats covering much of the Arctic shade the soil and slow that process. Soil scientist Ingrid Halvorsen and colleagues surveyed five tundra sites and concluded that thick moss can more than offset a warmer summer: ______
PROMPT: Which choice most effectively uses data from the table to complete the text?
A) Site A had the thinnest moss layer, at 2 centimeters, and the deepest active layer, at 78 centimeters.
B) Site C, the coolest site at 6.4 °C, had the shallowest active layer of the five, at 46 centimeters.
C) Site D, the warmest site at 9.5 °C, had the thickest moss layer and the shallowest active layer.
D) Site E, with a moss layer of 8 centimeters, had the shallowest active layer of the five, at 63 centimeters.
CORRECT: C
RATIONALE:
Choice C is the best answer because Site D had the highest mean summer air temperature, 9.5 °C, yet also the thickest moss layer, 18 centimeters, and the shallowest active layer, 46 centimeters. A warm site that nevertheless thawed the least is exactly what Halvorsen and colleagues' conclusion predicts.
Choice A is incorrect. This accurately describes data from the table, but it doesn't support the conclusion, which concerns moss offsetting a warmer summer. Site A's mean summer air temperature of 7.2 °C was not among the warmest, so this pairing says nothing about temperature being offset.
Choice B is incorrect. This choice misreads the table. Site C's active-layer depth was 71 centimeters, the second deepest of the five; 46 centimeters is Site D's active-layer depth.
Choice D is incorrect because, according to the table, Site E's active-layer depth of 63 centimeters is not the shallowest of the five sites; the shallowest is Site D's 46 centimeters.
